# One-year prices decide forty-year risk tiers

The year-0 near-tie is mostly neither a flat objective nor table noise. On every household whose tables are calibrated it is a **structural inconsistency**: the product's free-switching tables price the opening as one year's effect, while the 0.001 margin, applied from year 0 to paths that all start in the plan's tiers, holds the result for decades. On S126 and bridge 4 under the reader, and on S194, all 11 near-tie swaps realised **+0.39 to +0.64 survival points, always with the tables' sign, about 5 to 11 times the gap**, and tables that price the tier as held for life come within 0.79 to 0.97 of what holding realises [record]. That is the "one-step" heuristic that Brown and Smith (2011) expect to trade too little and find beaten by horizon-aware variants [literature]. Artefacts produce the largest swings: where the product's bridge reads miss survival by tens of points (share 0.95, S360), the misread manufactures a flat, near-doomed table, and near-tie swaps realise +13 or −6.1 points [record]. Genuine flatness appears only where it is harmless (S380 at 60 points); I put structural causes at 0.43, artefacts at 0.40 and genuine flatness at 0.17 [judgement]. The decisive test is a hold-by-resolution factorial (two openings forced on common paths under the shipping chooser, the same tables at margin 0, and the candidate, read against 30x5 and 60x5 tables) for 28 solves and roughly 20 to 25 core-hours; it designs no opening rule, as register row O67 requires. For the product, the code reframes ask 5: it **already keeps the plan's tier at any gap of 0.001 or less** [code], so the question is what guards that default, and the answer is a fortified two-opening rollout of the kind the planned "This year's actions" screen already specifies, plus a near-tie message in numbers, not a looser margin.

### Three regimes, not two

The record answers the brief's binary question with three regimes [record]. **Where tables are calibrated**, all 11 near-tie swaps on S126 and bridge 4 under the reader and on S194 realised +0.39 to +0.64 points, every one with the tables' sign, 4.8 to 11 times the gap ([7ab](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/ff13828/research/solver/results-7ab.txt#L46-L55), [7w](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/ff13828/research/solver/results-7w.txt#L60-L68)). That is not flatness, which needs the realised difference and the gap both near zero, and it is not noise, whose signs would vary. **Where the product's tables misread survival**, differences are large and of either sign. Share 0.95's shipping table reads 0.01% survival against 73.3% simulated, and its near-tie de-risk saves 392 of 3,000 paths (+13.1 points) at a gap of 4.3e-5. S360's table reads 0.94% against 34.3%, and its de-risk loses 497 paths and saves 9 of 8,000 (−6.1 points) at a gap of 5.6e-4 ([7af](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/ff13828/research/solver/results-7af.txt#L76-L105), [7ab](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/ff13828/research/solver/results-7ab.txt#L55)). **Where the objective really is flat**, the evidence says so: at 60 points, S380's two forced openings agree in sign in every world and net +0.28 ± 0.23 points against a table +0.10, and the de-risked opening that the 30-point log-odds read chose did not survive refinement ([B1](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/ff13828/research/solver/results-negrid-b1.txt#L88-L91)); S330's three rows read about 0, 0 and +0.5.

The structural regime carries the main weight on calibrated households, and one record pins it down [record]. When 7x compared the de-risked and plan's tiers each held for life, close to the hold the chooser executes, with tables solved for that hold, table and realised differences agreed at ratios of **0.79 (S126), 0.97 (S194) and 0.85 (bridge 4)**. The same construction gave 0.21 on share 0.95 and −0.03 on S360, where the bridge read is wrong ([7x](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/ff13828/research/solver/results-7x.txt#L81-L90)). Pricing the hold reconciles the calibrated households and cannot reconcile the misread ones; the 28 Sep review called the product's pricing of its own opening, at a tenth to a fifth of what it realises, "the cleanest self-inconsistency measured so far" ([review](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/ff13828/research/solver/deep-review-log.md?plain=1#L29)). The binary therefore misframes ask 1 [judgement]: the near-indifference is real, and small by construction, only in the tables' own model; the executed policy is not indifferent at all; and only on misread bridge households is the gap mostly noise.

### Seven mechanisms the record has not named

First, **the near-tie is an identity, not an anomaly** [derived]. With free switching, next year's value does not depend on the tier held, so the year-0 gap is exactly one year's return difference pushed through V₁: to second order V₁′Δμ + ½V₁″Δ(μ² + σ²), two terms of opposite sign and similar size, giving gaps of about 1e-4 at V₁′ = |V₁″| = 0.1. The ratio of realised to table difference then measures how long the hold lasts; the toy gives price-to-realised ratios of 0.13–0.19 under long holds against 1.02–1.06 at margin 0 [toy] ([run1b](../research_notes/Opening%20risk%20tier%20near%20ties/mechanisms/run1b_output.txt)).

Second, **a misread manufactures flatness** [derived]. Survival is flat across tiers wherever every tier gives about 0 or 1, so a table that reads a household as near-doomed produces a tiny gap whatever the truth; share 0.95 (read at 0.01%) and S360 (0.9%) are that case.

Third, **the candidate's knife edges are forced by selection** [derived]. With the tier in the state and a charge c in both passes, V(·, h) ≥ V(·, h′) − c, so a de-risked opening's net advantage is at most δ₁, one year's effect under the stay continuation. De-risked openings occur only in (c, c + δ₁], so clustering within 10% of the threshold follows whenever δ₁ < 1e-4: item 2 is evidence of small one-year effects, not of noise.

Fourth, **the year-0 thresholds are identical** [code]. Every path starts in the plan's tiers, the product's margin applies in year 0, and the candidate subtracts its charge from every leaving move, so both leave only when the best leaving score beats the best staying one by more than 0.001 ([solve.js](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/ff13828/src/solver/solve.js#L1393-L1412)). The candidate's "margin 0" never freed its opening; the score compared differs.

Fifth, **grid perturbations measure dispersion, not bias** [toy]. Both toys find the 30-point gap carries a cumulative bias, of one sign within each toy (the interpolation space sets which), several times its spread across node shifts: 2.8–4.9e-3 against 1.2–1.8e-3 in one, 6.2e-3 against 1.7e-3 in the other, where shifted grids flipped 1 to 3 of 30 openings while the 30-point choice disagreed with the fine one on 15 to 21 ([exp34](../research_notes/Opening%20risk%20tier%20near%20ties/toy/results_exp34.txt)). Resolution must be measured against a refined reference.

Sixth, **item 5's 21% is partly a snap** [code]: it compares the chooser's move with the move stored at the nearest cell. Seventh, **auxiliary terms live at the gap's scale** [derived]: one year's trim to 0.8 costs 8.9e-4 and one raise to 1.1 earns about 9.5e-4, so terms other than survival partly decide near-ties, and no committed file splits the year-0 gaps into their parts [record].

On item 4, the brief's signature (a sign-blind part that is not monotone in the step) needs several kinks inside one step. As the opening state moves one cell, each quadrature point crosses about one of next year's max-then-interpolate kinks, so 5 nodes × 3 worlds × 2 tiers give about 30 kinks of mixed sign per cell [derived]. The toy reproduces the signature without snaps or a reader [toy], which supports O67's ranking of interpolation and argmax kinks (B2) above the snaps. That ranking covers only the smaller part the bundle shares with P; the record attributes the bundle's larger sign-blind part to the margin or charge [record], which fits the derivation that a margin used as a backward rule makes values jump by m where the gain crosses it, while a charge leaves only kinks [derived].

### Four numbers per household separate the regimes cheaply

Four quantities do it [derived]: the table gap g; an exactly integrated year-0 re-score at the true state, which needs no solve (next year's interpolant integrated against each world's normal, or high-order quadrature in the product's log-odds space); the gap's resolution ρ, taken against one refined 60x5 solve rather than node shifts alone; and the paired common-random-number difference D between the two forced openings under the shipping margin and under margin 0, which needs no solve either.

The record has read the first row on its near-tie swaps, and the second only at the poor world's node (7ae, P) [record]. At 8,000 pairs, rollouts resolve the calibrated households' 0.4–0.6 points at z of about 5 to 7, and S360 at about 22 [derived].

## The literature puts the friction inside the Bellman equation

### Optimal switching prices the cost where the product leaves it out

[literature] In optimal-switching theory the friction sits inside the Bellman system. Regime values satisfy v_i ≥ max_j(v_j − H_ij), switching is optimal exactly where that holds with equality, and a positive round-trip cost keeps the switching regions apart ([Bayraktar & Egami 2010](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/0707.0100); [Djehiche, Hamadène & Popier](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0707.2663) for the finite-horizon, multi-regime case). Hysteresis matters "even for small irreversibility" ([Dixit 1991](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/restud/v58y1991i1p141-151..html)).

The closest analogue to the product is [Brown & Smith (2011)](https://jimsmith.host.dartmouth.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Dynamic_Portfolio_Optimization_with_Transaction_Costs.pdf). Their "one-step" heuristic uses the frictionless value function as its continuation and charges costs only in the current period, and they expected such strategies to "trade too little" because they "underestimate the benefits of moving toward the optimal position". Heuristics that scale the cost by how long a position lasts, or that optimise with costs over a horizon, came within **0.09–0.29 points of certainty-equivalent return** of a dual bound. A finite horizon also makes the policy horizon-dependent and widens the no-trade band towards the end ([Liu & Loewenstein 2002](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v15y2002i3p805-835.html)), which a margin held constant for 40 years cannot do.

[derived] The product's margin is the one-step heuristic in discrete form: a lump hurdle of 0.001 set every year against a one-year flow of benefit. A tier is held while each year's one-year gain stays below the hurdle, hence the record's 0.03–0.04 switches a path on S126 and bridge 4 (0.78 on S194, 0.18 on share 0.95) [record]. The candidate is the textbook form, in which a wrong opening costs at most one year's exposure plus one switch. In a diffusion approximation the inaction band's half-width is (3σ²c/2a)^(1/3), consistent with the ε^(1/3) no-trade law ([Mayerhofer 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.07100)): wide relative to c, costing of order c^(2/3), and collapsing at c = 0 into chattering, the record's 5 switches a path at margin 0 on 30 points [record].

### A posteriori bounds are computable but cannot resolve 1e-3 over forty years

[literature] [Singh & Yee (1994)](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/teaching/cis620/papers/SinghYee.pdf) bound a greedy policy's loss by 2γε/(1−γ) from a sup-norm value error computable through the Bellman residual, and the performance-difference lemma ([Kakade & Langford 2002](https://mlanthology.org/icml/2002/kakade2002icml-approximately/)) sums the loss along the policy's own paths. Information-relaxation duality certifies a heuristic against an upper bound built from approximate values ([Brown, Smith & Sun 2010](https://jimsmith.host.dartmouth.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Information_Relaxations_and_Duality_in_Stochastic_Dynamic_Programs.pdf)). [Farahmand (2011)](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2011/file/013d407166ec4fa56eb1e1f8cbe183b9-Paper.pdf) shows that a wrong greedy choice needs an error of at least half the action gap and costs at most the gap. [derived] Over a finite horizon a uniform off-grid residual r adds up to about 1,560r of loss bound over 40 years, so certifying 1e-3 needs r of about 6e-7, far beyond a 30-point grid. Every such bound is also against the optimum of the model the tables solve, not a chooser that holds, and the product's per-world tables are themselves a wait-and-see relaxation (Σ_w p_w max ≥ max Σ_w p_w) whose excess differs by action. Farahmand's protection holds only when both passes use the same switching rule and the tables embed no choice the chooser cannot make. The usable resolution measures are empirical: per-opening table-versus-rollout bias, the gap's spread against a refined reference, and off-grid residuals along paths [judgement].

### Rollouts on common paths beat table-side remedies, within their resolution

[literature] Rollout is one policy-improvement step evaluated by simulation. With exact values it cannot worsen the base policy, and its "fortified" form keeps the incumbent unless something better is found ([Bertsekas](https://www.mit.edu/~dimitrib/Rollouts_Survey.pdf)). Common random numbers cut the samples that ranking-and-selection procedures need ([Nelson & Matejcik 1995](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v41y1995i12p1935-1945.html)). Indifference-zone guarantees, though, are silent exactly when the top two tie ([Eckman & Henderson 2018](https://eckman.engr.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/233/2020/01/PGS-overview-paper.pdf)), and picking the best of three noisy estimates disappoints by about 85% of their standard deviation ([Smith & Winkler 2006](https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/2006-smith.pdf)).

[derived] At the root alone, choosing the opening with the best simulated value under the actual chooser, with that chooser's own opening among the candidates, cannot do worse than the chooser, and needs no consistency condition. A fortified version keeps the plan's tier unless the one-sided lower bound on the paired difference clears a margin, which caps the chance of ending worse than the plan's tier at α. The estimate should be reported from fresh paths to avoid the optimizer's curse. Resolution is the binding limit: at 8,000 pairs the 95% half-width is 0.15–0.54 points for discordance of 0.5–6%, while a 1e-3 gap needs 39,000 to 471,000 pairs and a 1e-4 gap needs millions.

The other remedies fare worse [literature]. Logit or entropy-regularised choice removes argmax flips and smooths discrete-choice kinks ([Iskhakov et al. 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/quante/v8y2017i2p317-365.html); [Geist et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.11275)) at a bias of at most Tσ·log K [derived], but a deterministic version needs a blended allocation, a new product action, and leaves interpolation kinks and snaps in place. Minimax regret is menu-dependent ([Stoye 2009](https://stoye.economics.cornell.edu/docs/stoye-joe2009-pre.pdf)), and across grids it needs a solve per variant, which gate 5 rules out at run time. Status-quo rules have a decision-theoretic basis: with a set of admissible models, an agent leaves the status quo only for an alternative better under all of them ([Bewley 2002](https://faculty.econ.ucdavis.edu/faculty/nehring/teaching/econ203/readings/bewley-knightian-decision-theory.pdf); [Ortoleva 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/gamebe/v69y2010i2p411-424.html)). Defaults stick and read as advice: 71% of auto-enrolled employees kept both the default rate and the default fund ([Madrian & Shea 2001](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7682)).

[toy] In the second toy, re-ranking the top two openings by 8,000 paired rollouts removed the coarse grid's harm in every pipeline (mean loss 1–2e-4, worst 2.2e-3); thresholds up to 1e-3 and unanimity over nine grids barely helped, because the error is a common bias; and **always keeping the plan's tier was the worst rule**, with mean losses of 3.1–7.7e-3 ([exp34](../research_notes/Opening%20risk%20tier%20near%20ties/toy/results_exp34.txt)). That last finding needs a caveat [judgement]: the toy's tier gaps were about ten times the product's and its best opening was de-risked in 9 to 15 of 30 households, so its magnitudes do not transfer. Its direction does, since the record's calibrated households lose 0.4–0.6 points whenever the plan's tier is kept [record]. What the toy cannot show is S360, where leaving on a misread table costs 6.1 points. That is Bewley's case for an incumbent, and a fortified rollout reconciles the two: the plan's tier stays the default, and only simulation, never the table, can overturn it.

### Lifecycle models re-optimise freely or price stickiness inside the optimisation

[literature] Mainstream lifecycle and decumulation models re-optimise the risky share every year, with no friction ([Cocco, Gomes & Maenhout 2005](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v18y2005i2p491-533.html); [Forsyth 2022](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2008.06598), searching a discretised control grid exhaustively). Where stickiness appears it comes from costs inside the Bellman equation, such as participation costs ([Vissing-Jørgensen 2002](https://www.nber.org/papers/w8884)) or transaction and information costs ([Abel, Eberly & Panageas 2013](https://econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/2013/07/01/optimal-inattention-stock-market-information-costs-and)), and the resulting policies are bands, not permanent holds. Pre-committed glide paths barely beat a constant mix while adaptive strategies "significantly outperform" ([Forsyth & Vetzal 2019](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~paforsyt/adaptive_vs_det.pdf)), and the case for de-risking early then re-risking ([Pfau & Kitces 2014](https://www.financialplanningassociation.org/article/journal/JAN14-reducing-retirement-risk-rising-equity-glide-path)) is contested ([Estrada, secondary summary](https://cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/global-stocks-bonds-glidepath-during-retirement)); a held de-risk forfeits the re-risk leg either way. Forsyth (2022) meets the planner's near-tie directly: where "the control only weakly effects the objective function", he adds 1e-6 times terminal wealth, leaving summary statistics unchanged to four digits. [judgement] No published model holds a discrete tier with an execution margin on frictionless tables. The candidate's form is the standard one, and if users revise rarely, that belongs in the model as a cost or a review interval, not in a margin.

## Structural inconsistency carries a plurality; misreads cause the largest swings

The credences in the opening table start from the mechanisms notes' draft (hold 0.30, resolution 0.28, flatness 0.12, clairvoyance 0.10, auxiliary terms 0.07, cliff 0.06, snaps 0.04, menu order 0.03) and move on the record's evidence. Every credence is a [judgement]; the evidence behind each is tagged.

**The hold rises to 0.36:** all 11 calibrated swaps carry its signature, and held-for-life tables reconcile them at 0.79–0.97 [record]; it applies to the product and to TS+J run with the margin as a rule, not to the candidate, whose charge prices the hold. **Misreads become their own cause at 0.20,** split out of resolution: they produce every swing above one point in the record (11 of 26 near-tie rows) and manufacture the near-tie itself [record]. They are mainly the product's bridge reads; the candidate's weaker form is S364, whose reader tables prefer a de-risked opening that, against the shipping product, loses 31 paths and saves none [record]. **Resolution falls to 0.16:** it decides on which side of 0.001 a gap lands (the product's gap moves −14% and +28% across grids; the candidate's share 0.95 opening flips), but the realised sign never varied across the calibrated swaps, and under P bridge 4 and S194 open alike at all three grids [record]. It applies to both versions, with a bias larger than any node-shift spread [toy]. **Genuine flatness drops to 0.10:** it explains why gaps are small but is harmless on its own. **Per-world continuation falls to 0.07:** its grade-B decomposition (21 of 25 lost paths recovered) comes from tier-state arms on two households [record]. Auxiliary terms fall to 0.04 because item 7 is mostly margin comparisons, the cliff to 0.03 because it is where differences land rather than why, and snaps and menu order to 0.02 each, because snaps act only through next year's reads and the tie tolerance moves tax, not tiers [code].

By family: structural 0.43, artefact 0.40, genuine flatness 0.17. Structural inconsistency carries the plurality and all of the calibrated households' harm; artefacts carry nearly as much weight and the largest single swings. The candidate removes most structural causes by construction, so its live question is resolution against genuine flatness, and the record leans towards small, real effects [record]: TS+J's mixture swaps realised +0.03 to +0.34 points, and the reviews put the poor-world under-pricing behind item 6's ratio of 0.74 (0.738 again under P) at about 0.02 points a household.

## A hold-by-resolution factorial decides between the three

### Why a factorial rather than the resolution audit

Of the mechanisms notes' three designs, the hold-by-resolution factorial with paired rollouts is the one whose outcome changes a decision [judgement]. The resolution audit measures only ρ, and since both toys show node-shift dispersion understating the bias, its central quantity is a lower bound; the record already shows the realised sign stable on calibrated households. ## The product already keeps the plan's tier; guard it rather than loosen it

### The code has already answered the first option

[code] The product applies its 0.001 margin in year 0, and every path starts in the plan's tiers, so any year-0 gap of 0.001 or less already keeps the plan's tier ([solve.js](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/ff13828/src/solver/solve.js#L1546-L1571)). "Keep the plan's tier below a stated resolution" is therefore the shipping rule, with 0.001 as the stated resolution. My recommendation is a guarded status quo [judgement]. **Keep the plan's tier at gaps of 0.001 or less, and do not move that threshold yet:** lowering it de-risks S360 into a 6.1-point loss, raising it forgoes more S194-type gains, and the gap's own grid movement (−14% to +28%) [record] already puts its uncertainty near the threshold at about 3e-4 [derived], with the toys saying the true bias is larger [toy]. Bewley-style inertia is the right posture while the admissible models disagree, and the maintainer should record its price: about 0.4–0.6 points on calibrated near-ties such as S194 and up to 13 on share 0.95-type misreads [record]. **Guard it with the fortified rollout that Phase 9 needs anyway:** simulate both openings under the shipping chooser on a fixed, reserved seed, and switch only when the paired lower bound clears the programme's margin at Holm-adjusted α, the pattern the 'auto' rule already follows with a zero margin. **Tell the user, in numbers, when the rollout cannot separate the openings,** with three states (clear; equivalent, only when a TOST passes; inconclusive), per-1,000 frequencies rounded to the rollout's resolution, and "we have kept your chosen level". Numerical uncertainty costs little trust ([van der Bles et al. 2020](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/304699)), and the Consumer Duty expects tested communications ([FCA FG22/5](https://fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg22-5.pdf)). Smoothing, grid unanimity and minimax regret stay out, for the reasons in the table, and each change above needs a registered test and the maintainer's decision.

The rollout's power is the limit to state plainly [derived]. At the product's one-core allowance, 62 s per 1,000 path-runs buys about 470–760 pairs: enough for share 0.95's 13% discordance and S360's 6% at z above 5, not for S194's +0.53, whose 0.54% discordance yields about 3 or 4 discordant pairs. The candidate's 165–175 s of four-core wall-clock would buy about 5,000 pairs, if its forward runs parallelise and cost what the product's do (neither is measured). That would resolve one-sided 0.5-point effects and the upper end of TS+J's +0.03 to +0.34 near-tie effects, the nearest measured analogue for the candidate, but not the lower end.

Whether gate 5 counts these forward runs is the maintainer's definitional call: the literature strand puts them outside the gate because they need no solve, while the code strand shows they are real wall-clock time, and the gate's wording ("a solve ... takes at most 25% more time") does not settle it. Determinism needs a decision too [code]. Paths come from seed + i·7919 through mulberry32 and Box–Muller, deterministic on one engine, but one third-party report found different `Math.cos` bits in Node 20 and Chrome 149 ([bounce #8](https://codeberg.org/perplexdotgg/bounce/issues/8)), so a rollout choice on its threshold could differ across engines; a bit-exact normal generator would close that, at the cost of re-basing every seeded result [judgement].

## Conclusion

The gap is the wrong statistic. The product compares a one-year quantity and executes a lifetime one, so the year-0 gap says little about the stakes; the ratio of realised to table difference does, because it measures how long the hold lasts. The programme's unifying idea (O100: decisions on gaps below what the tables resolve) is half right: it describes the misread households, where a misread manufactures both the near-tie and the harm, but not the calibrated ones, where the tables get the sign right and the inconsistency does the damage. Neither half needs an opening rule. Consistency, already built into the candidate, removes the structural half; accurate bridge reads and resolution remove the artefact half; a rule designed from flips would treat a symptom of both.

For the shipping product, the safe design already exists twice in the codebase, in the 'auto' rule and the Phase 9 specification: an incumbent the user chose, challenged only by simulation on common paths. It catches the misreads, not the steady half-point the one-step structure leaves on every calibrated near-tie; recovering that is the candidate's job.
