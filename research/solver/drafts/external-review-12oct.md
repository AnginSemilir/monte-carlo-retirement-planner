# Two distinct faults drive bridge-year survival misreads

The bridge-year misreads have two separate causes, and only one of them is the wealth grid. S380's "too high when poor, too low when rich" pattern, its flattened spread and LOR's reading above its own simulation all come from blending along log wealth: at S380's reads the reader's p·c + R correction is worth at most about 0.03 points, so its bridge read is a plain probability blend of solved survival smeared through eight bridge years, and NE-GRID B1 held all four items as the wealth axis doubled (BASE's errors went from +4.2/−2.1/−2.5 to **+0.15/−1.39/−0.93**, its spread ratio from 0.90 to about 0.98, and LOR's over-read disappeared). S364's preference for a de-risked opening, and its table falling as the world improves while the same policy's simulated survival rises, have a different cause, best supported by the records and the toy model though not yet by logged corner values. A share cell spanning the whole bridge cliff inflates every world's plain blend; NE-ROOT's corner shares imply at least 12 and 26 points in the average and good worlds against 0.30% and 1.55% simulated. The clip-and-copy split removes that inflation mainly through corners that have crossed the p_k = 0.5 switch, whose stencil weight rises with the world (0, 0.17, 0.49), so the table falls as the world improves and can reverse rankings, which smoothing alone cannot do. Beneath both sits an **under-read of roughly 1–2.7 points that also appears in S224**, which has no bridge years, so the reader cannot be its whole cause. On the five review questions: (1) more wealth points is a convergence check, not a ground truth, and the off-grid one-step residual plus a cliff-aligned local grid are cleaner references; (2) the pension-share coordinate is the problem for households near or below their bridge need, where share nodes placed by accessible money over remaining floor need beat any change of blend, but not for S380; (3) p·c + R is sound algebra that does nothing where p is uniformly near 0 or 1 and is fragile where corner chances straddle 0.5; (4) the forced-opening test is a valid self-consistency and rollout check but not an optimality test, and its control guards the copy rule's rescue of the dead corner rather than accuracy on the cliff; (5) the biggest further suspect is the under-read outside the bridge. NE-GRID C will settle the wealth order and the quadrature, share and path-noise parts of that under-read, but not the copy switch or the coordinate, so before spending its roughly 25 core-hours the maintainer should log the per-corner split that every bridge read already computes, add copy-threshold and cliff-aligned share arms, and register C1 as a deterministic three-grid order test on table values.

## S380's misread was wealth blur, and B1 has already removed most of it

Each claim below carries a tag for the kind of evidence behind it: [record] for the programme's own results files, register rows and reviews; [code] for what the source on `claude/project-access-i58m0x` at 8654333 actually does; [derivation] for the researchers' own analysis, with arithmetic done for this review on recorded numbers marked "this review"; [toy] for runs of the researchers' toy bridge model, whose mechanisms transfer to the solver but whose magnitudes do not; and [literature] for published work.

Everything here concerns the research candidate: reader on, with tierState, jointWorlds, `bridgeStep 'exact'`, the switch charge and allowance-bucket interpolation. It does not concern the product, which still ships `bridgeRead: false` ([solve.js L1874–1879](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/86543335cd7e9aa4629306ad3eb3c6badfc01838/src/solver/solve.js#L1874-L1879)).

For the funded household, how the reader is built matters far less than the brief assumes. [code] `readValues` reassembles the read as p(x)·Σw c + Σw R, where R_k = S_k − p_k c_k ([grid.js L407–417](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/86543335cd7e9aa4629306ad3eb3c6badfc01838/src/solver/grid.js#L407-L417), [reader.js L134](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/86543335cd7e9aa4629306ad3eb3c6badfc01838/src/solver/reader.js#L134)). Algebraically this is **the probability-linear blend of solved survival plus a correction, Σw_k S_k + Σw_k c_k (p(x) − p_k)**. The deep review records the same identity ([deep-review-log L151](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/86543335cd7e9aa4629306ad3eb3c6badfc01838/research/solver/deep-review-log.md?plain=1#L151)), and the toy confirms it to 3×10⁻¹⁶ ([toy exp1](../research_notes/Bridge%20year%20survival%20reader%20review/toy/out/exp1_3_reads.txt)). The correction can act only where the query's chance differs from its corners' chances.

[derivation] The researchers recomputed p from reader.js's formulas in a scratch calculation, not a repo record, at S380's inputs: £712.5k accessible, about £42k of floor bills a year, eight bills. That gives **p ≥ 0.999997 at the opening in every world** and p ≥ 0.9997 even after a −2.86σ first year. The quadrature-weighted correction at the year-0 decision is at most about 0.03 points on both the 30- and 60-point stencils ([code facts §10](../research_notes/Bridge%20year%20survival%20reader%20review/solver_code_facts.md)). The toy agrees: 97% of S380-like path reads have every corner above p = 0.99 ([toy exp1](../research_notes/Bridge%20year%20survival%20reader%20review/toy/out/exp1_3_reads.txt)). So S380's bridge reads are a plain probability blend of S, and its symptoms belong to that blend rather than to the split.

That blend has a predictable bias. [derivation] A linear blend over-reads where the function being blended is convex and under-reads where it is concave. For a lognormal-type cliff, that means too high below roughly one half and too low above. Averaged over grid offsets, the read equals the truth convolved with a triangular kernel of variance H²/6. Each backward step therefore adds numerical variance that behaves like extra return volatility, pulls every world towards one half and compresses the spread across worlds ([interpolation note §1](../research_notes/Bridge%20year%20survival%20reader%20review/interpolation_and_grids.md)).

[derivation] From grid.js's axis rule, S380's wealth nodes sit about 28% apart at 30 points and 13% apart at 60 ([grid.js L96–98](https://github.com/AnginSemilir/monte-carlo-retirement-planner/blob/86543335cd7e9aa4629306ad3eb3c6badfc01838/src/solver/grid.js#L96-L98)). [derivation, this review] On the note's H²/6 formula, each read then adds numerical variance of roughly **0.6 of a year's total-wealth return variance at 30 points and 0.15 at 60** (taking about 13% volatility from the code's tier table and S380's mix), compounded through eight bridge years.

[literature] Semi-Lagrangian schemes, which read values off-node every step just as a backward pass does, carry an interpolation term of order Δx^{r+1}/Δt, consistent with an error paid once per step and accumulated over the horizon ([Ferretti](https://www.mat.uniroma1.it/ricerca/convegni/2014/Maurizio60/PDF/Ferretti.pdf)). Multilinear interpolation is an averager, a max-norm non-expansion, so its per-step errors add rather than amplify ([Gordon 1995](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pabbeel/cs287-fa13/optreadings/Gordon-1995.pdf)). [toy] The toy reproduces the sign pattern and converges at about second order in the wealth step; its funded control household goes −0.48 → −0.10 → 0.00 weighted at 30/60/120 points ([toy exp7](../research_notes/Bridge%20year%20survival%20reader%20review/toy/out/exp7_convergence.txt)).

## The addendum (the maintainer, 12 Oct 12:57 UK), verbatim

**Addendum to the bridge-year reader review**
1. **Probit arm (near-free):** blend the reader's corners in probit space. In the toy, with the policy fixed, this cut S380's weighted error from −0.79 to +0.22 points on the same grid. Storing corner values already transformed keeps read cost flat. The report mentions probit only in passing; consider it alongside rank 2.
2. **Wealth nodes placed at a fixed count:** cluster the 30–40 wealth points where survival changes, rather than spreading them evenly in log terms from 0.1 to 60+ years of spend. It's untested here, but it's an alternative to a uniform 60 within gate 5's limit of about 40.
3. **Runtime of the accessible ÷ need share nodes:** only bridge years need them. The toy needed about 12, so an 8-year bridge adds roughly a fifth to solve time (an unmeasured estimate).
