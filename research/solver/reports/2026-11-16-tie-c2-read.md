# The improved couples' tie rule, read (16 Nov): PASSES, and the rule takes LOOKHOLD

EXPLORATORY, seed 7001 (D105; `TIE-C2.md`, its rule committed before any build). The read whole is `results-tie-c2.txt`
(`reduce-tie-c2.mjs`). Beside it, written after the read and deciding nothing: `results-tie-c2-beside.txt`
(`tie-c2-beside.mjs`: each arm's engine time by unit, for the speed exception). Every figure below is printed in one of
those two files.

The run: twelve couples of the couples' re-run panel not used before, 8,000 paths each, 45 points, estate weight 0.02. It ran
through the launcher from the main tree at b6c71a19 (code c0dcfa9ef2f2, audit fdc4d7871131), 15 Nov 19:33 UK to 16 Nov
00:30 UK, four units at a time. The gate is fair: one code and one audit in every unit, seed 7001 and 8,000 paths in every
arm, the couples' candidate stage C ran (af001697ce1b), each arm's options read back as named, and each person's tables the
same before the first arm and after the last.

## In plain words

Above both partners' estate caps, the solver gives a couple's score nothing for a larger pot. So among choices that score
almost the same, it has no reason to prefer the one that leaves more. The first tie rule (`TIE-C.md`, 15 Nov) took the
least tax this year, and failed on one couple, S311F: its picks led to a state where a partner's table read billions as the
top of its range, and the tables then stepped that partner's risk down. This run tried two remedies against today's
couples' candidate (CANDS):
- **HOLD:** a person whose wealth is more than twice the top of their table's range never steps down in risk.
- **LOOKHOLD:** the hold, plus the forward look. Near-ties above both caps are ranked by the household's pot at the plan's
  end on the median path, not by this year's tax.

**What it found:**
- **LOOKHOLD passes all eight parts.**
  - Survival is unchanged: pooled `+0.000` points (`-0.004` to `+0.004`), and no couple below the bar.
  - Spending delivered: `-0.0001%` pooled.
  - The capped pot: `-0.002%` pooled; no couple lower by more than `0.5%`.
  - The pot left on the typical path: `+0.288%` pooled (`+0.281` to `+0.296`). It is higher on all twelve couples, from
    `+0.007%` (S149) to `+1.018%` (S337F). No couple's average pound is below the bar.
  - Lifetime tax: `£370,735` to `£362,909` pooled (`-2.11%`). It is lower on eleven couples and higher on S329F
    (`£265,089` to `£270,607`), whose typical-path pot is `+0.430%` higher.
  - Most of the gain is the younger couples': the young cohort's eight `+0.409%` (`+0.398` to `+0.419`) on the typical
    path, tax `-2.15%`; the library's four `+0.048%` (`+0.043` to `+0.053`), tax `-1.25%`.
- **HOLD fails part 8: no gain.**
  - Its stored outcomes are CANDS's byte for byte: survival, the failure year, the pot, lifetime tax and spending
    delivered, on all `96,000` paths.
  - In the replays (the first 100 paths of each couple), a person is past the limit in some decisions along CANDS's path
    (S327F: 120 and 125 of 5,911). But no arm steps them down there, so the hold has nothing to stop. Along each arm's own
    path it acts at 0 decisions, and no person past the limit steps down.
  - So this run never tested the hold. LOOKHOLD's gain is the forward look's. Along LOOKHOLD's own path in the replays, the
    look changes the pick at 106 (S149) to 945 (S331F) decisions a couple.
- **So the rule takes LOOKHOLD**, by `TIE-C2.md`: "If one passes, that one is taken."
- **The speed exception does not apply.**
  - LOOKHOLD's engine time a path against CANDS's, the median over the twelve: `x1.219`, under the `x1.25` bar.
  - By couple it runs from `x0.96` (S149) to `x1.32` (S329F). Five couples are above `x1.25`: S329F, S331F, S333F, S337F
    and S341F. The library's four run near `x1.0` (`x0.96` to `x1.04`).
  - Each arm's engine time a path, the mean over the units: CANDS `164.4` ms, LOOKHOLD `196.3` ms.

**The timing, said beside the read** (`results-tie-c2-beside.txt`):
- The engine time is a wall clock, four units at a time.

  Through that window the session was working on the share rules' registration: the first design review's fix, the

## What it decides

- By D105 ("1 - pass goes straight into default") and `TIE-C2.md`'s PASSES, the ruling naming this run: LOOKHOLD becomes
  the couples' default, without another word. `src/solverSettings.js` `APP_COUPLE` gains `tieJoint: 1e-4`,
  `tieLook: true` and `holdPast: 2`, and the page's solve of a couple carries them into its joint move.
- The decided-defaults block and `couple-default.test.mjs` change in the same commit.
- The reviewer looks at the change of default before it is committed. It goes to `main` with the next batch, on B1's gate.

## What it cannot show

- Twelve couples, one seed and one estate weight. Each held 60/40, so each was solved at the half share.
- Every couple was entered elder first, and every arm ran the whole menu, not the speed mode's shortlist.
- The forward look uses one median path, as a guide. It is not the expected end pot.
- The hold never acted, so this run says nothing of it on real couples.
  - It goes in because the rule takes the arm whole.
  - What stands for it is its test (`couple-tie-look.test.mjs`) and the pre-launch check on S311F's two paths. With the
    allowance for noise, a loss like S311F's would pass part 5(b), so the hold is what removes that step, by construction
    (`TIE-C2.md`, What it cannot show).
- The tables are the same in every arm, so a couple's solve moves only in the paths the page runs: `x1.20` to `x1.32` a
  path on the younger eight, `x0.96` to `x1.04` on the library's four.
