## In plain words

Above both partners' estate caps, the solver gives a couple's score nothing for a larger pot. So among choices that score
almost the same, it has no reason to prefer the one that leaves more. The first tie rule (`TIE-C.md`, 15 Nov) took the
least tax this year, and failed on one couple, S311F: its picks led to a state where a partner's table read billions as the
top of its range, and the tables then stepped that partner's risk down. This run tried two remedies against today's
couples' candidate (CANDS):
- **HOLD:** a person whose wealth is more than twice the top of their table's range never steps down in risk.
- **LOOKHOLD:** the hold, plus the forward look. Near-ties above both caps are ranked by the household's pot at the plan's
  end on the median path, not by this year's tax.

**What it found:**
- **LOOKHOLD passes all eight parts.**
  - Survival is unchanged: pooled `+0.000` points (`-0.004` to `+0.004`), and no couple below the bar.
  - Spending delivered: `-0.0001%` pooled.
  - The capped pot: `-0.002%` pooled; no couple lower by more than `0.5%`.
  - The pot left on the typical path: `+0.288%` pooled (`+0.281` to `+0.296`). It is higher on all twelve couples, from
    `+0.007%` (S149) to `+1.018%` (S337F). No couple's average pound is below the bar.
  - Lifetime tax: `£370,735` to `£362,909` pooled (`-2.11%`). It is lower on eleven couples and higher on S329F
    (`£265,089` to `£270,607`), whose typical-path pot is `+0.430%` higher.
  - Most of the gain is the younger couples': the young cohort's eight `+0.409%` (`+0.398` to `+0.419`) on the typical
    path, tax `-2.15%`; the library's four `+0.048%` (`+0.043` to `+0.053`), tax `-1.25%`.
- **HOLD fails part 8: no gain.**
  - Its stored outcomes are CANDS's byte for byte: survival, the failure year, the pot, lifetime tax and spending
    delivered, on all `96,000` paths. So HOLD's hold never acted, on any path.
  - In the replays (the first 100 paths of each couple), a person is past the limit in some decisions along CANDS's path
    (S327F: 120 and 125 of 5,911), but no arm steps them down there. Along each arm's own path the hold acts at 0
    decisions, and no person past the limit steps down.
  - In LOOKHOLD's own arm the hold's acts are counted on those 100 paths alone (the audit does not count them on its
    engine's 8,000), so on its other 7,900 paths a couple, whether the hold acted is not known.
  - In the rule's sense the gain is the forward look's: LOOKHOLD against HOLD, the hold in both, is the look's own effect.
    Along LOOKHOLD's own path in the replays, the look changes the pick at 106 (S149) to 945 (S331F) decisions a couple.
- **So the rule takes LOOKHOLD**, by `TIE-C2.md`: "If one passes, that one is taken."
- **By the rule's measure the speed exception does not apply, but the bar lies within the timing's own order effect**
  (section A):
  - The rule reads LOOKHOLD's engine time a path against CANDS's, the median over the twelve, at `x1.219`, under the
    `x1.25` bar. By couple it runs from `x0.96` (S149) to `x1.32` (S329F). Five couples are above `x1.25`.
  - The time is a wall clock, and each unit ran its arms in one order: CANDS, HOLD, LOOKHOLD, TIE4. On the quiet units 4
    to 11, HOLD reads `x0.987` of CANDS (seven of eight below one), though its outcomes are CANDS's byte for byte and it
    makes CANDS's every move. TIE4, which does the first rule's work on top, reads `x0.968` (seven of eight below one). So
    CANDS, run first, is timed slow by about 1% to 3% on a quiet machine.
  - With that effect undone, the readings straddle the bar:
    - rescaled by the quiet units' HOLD against CANDS: `x1.236`;
    - rescaled by their TIE4 against CANDS: `x1.260`;
    - LOOKHOLD against TIE4, both run after the first arm: `x1.255`.
  - The younger eight read `x1.259` against CANDS (`x1.271` against TIE4); there the look changes the pick at 684 to 945
    decisions a couple. The library's four read `x0.958` to `x1.038`, at 106 to 201. The median over the twelve falls below
    the bar through those four.
  - Units 0 to 3 ran their first two arms while the session worked on the machine (four commits, git log: 19:58 to
    20:23 UK); there HOLD reads `x1.040` of CANDS. Set above every other couple, they bound the median at `x1.240` against
    CANDS, `x1.241` against HOLD and `x1.292` against TIE4. The first write-up gave only the first of these, and claimed it
    held "however much load": it bounds the load on units 0 to 3, not the order effect, which is in every unit.

## Beside the rule, deciding nothing

- **The gain is above the caps, at a small cost below them** (section B).
  - Over all `96,000` paths, LOOKHOLD's capped pot (each path's pot up to the household's cap) is lower by more than a
    pound on 990 paths and higher on 488: `£5,942,100` lost and `£4,086,176` gained, the pooled `-0.002%` of part 4.
  - Of LOOKHOLD's 11 paths ending more than a tenth lower, four end below the cap in both arms, with lifetime tax the same
    or higher: S327F path 5806 (`-13.4%`), S329F path 4759 (`-11.5%`), and S337F paths 3448 (`-23.7%`) and 4935
    (`-11.8%`). The look acts only where both partners are above their caps, so these are paths that fell below them later.
    The look ranks moves on the median path, not on a path that turns bad.
  - TIE4 has 8 such paths, none below the cap in both arms. Its capped pot is lower on 643 paths and higher on 558.
  - LOOKHOLD has 168 paths ending more than a tenth higher, 142 of them S337F's. Four of them end below the cap in both
    arms.
- **The pooled interval treats the twelve couples as independent, but the young eight share their paths** (section C).
  - They have the same horizon on seed 7001.
  - Taken by path, on the 7,101 paths where all twelve leave a pot in both arms, the typical path's change is `+0.306%`
    (`+0.295` to `+0.318`), its standard error `0.0059` against the reducer's `0.0037`.
  - Part 8's lower end stays well above nought. A later read with a close margin should take its interval by path.
- **TIE4, the first rule at the singles' margin, passes all eight parts on these couples too.**
  - The typical path `+0.237%` (`+0.231` to `+0.243`), tax `-3.22%`, and `x0.976` a path.
  - On these twelve no path decides part 5 as S311F's two did in the first read. The pre-launch check
    (`results-tie-c2-precheck.txt`) ran S311F's two deciding paths under each arm. TIE4 steps a person down past the limit
    at 2 decisions on each and loses `34.14%` and `16.76%` of the pot. HOLD and LOOKHOLD step down at none, their pots
    within `0.03%` of CANDS's.
- **The pooled tax without the couple carrying most of its change:** LOOKHOLD without S335F, `£282,270` to `£278,517`.

## What it decides, and what is yours

- **The rule's outcome:** PASSES, and LOOKHOLD is taken. By the rule's measure (`x1.219`) the speed exception does not
  apply, and by D105 LOOKHOLD would become the couples' default without another word.
- **But the exception's reading sits at the bar, within the timing's own order effect.** The exception is yours (D105: a
  taken arm adding more than a quarter to a couple's path comes back as the trade). So the change of default waits for
  your word, on one of:
  1. **LOOKHOLD becomes the default as the rule reads it.** `src/solverSettings.js` `APP_COUPLE` gains `tieJoint: 1e-4`,
     `tieLook: true` and `holdPast: 2`, carried by the page's solve of a couple into its joint move, with the
     decided-defaults block and `couple-default.test.mjs`. The reviewer looks at the change before it is committed.
  2. **The trade, weighed now.** LOOKHOLD's gain is `+0.288%` on the pot left on the typical path and `-2.11%` lifetime
     tax, with survival and spending unchanged. Against it, a couple's path takes about a quarter longer on the younger
     couples (`x1.20` to `x1.32`) and about the same on the library's four (`x0.96` to `x1.04`). The tables are unchanged,
     so only the paths the page runs take longer.
  3. **A timing with the order undone, first.** CANDS and LOOKHOLD run each in a fresh process, alternated, on a few
     younger couples, on a quiet machine: after the share rules' registered run frees the cores (about 15:00 UK).
     EXPLORATORY, deciding nothing itself; the figure then comes back to you with 1 or 2.

## What it cannot show

- Twelve couples, one seed and one estate weight. Each held 60/40, so each was solved at the half share.
- Every couple was entered elder first. Every arm ran the whole menu at the dial's position 1 with consent given, not the
  speed mode's shortlist and not a locked position.
  - At a locked position each person is kept to their base's tier pair, and the hold, as built, would put back a step
    down past the limit whatever the lock (`couple.js` `holdMove`). That is unmeasured here, for the change of default to
    settle.
- The forward look uses one median path, as a guide. It is not the expected end pot.
- The hold never acted on HOLD's paths, nor on the paths counted of LOOKHOLD's, so this run says nothing of it on real
  couples.
  - It goes in because the rule takes the arm whole.
  - What stands for it is its test (`couple-tie-look.test.mjs`) and the pre-launch check on S311F's two paths. With the
    allowance for noise, a loss like S311F's would pass part 5(b), so the hold is what removes that step, by construction
    (`TIE-C2.md`, What it cannot show).
