# The savings split on today's candidate, read (19 Nov): A GAIN AND A HARM, the gain in survival and spending

EXPLORATORY, seed 7002, deciding nothing (B7 in D51; D129; `B7-FACTORIALS.md` and its two amendments, committed before it
ran; `SAVINGS-SPLIT.md`; the read whole in `results-save.txt`, the figures beside it in `results-save-beside.txt`).

## In plain words

The savings split lets each working year's move choose where that year's saving goes, at the same net cost to the
household:
- the plan's own saving;
- the pension first (its tax relief taken), then the ISA;
- the ISA first.

Without the split, the plan's own saving stands. This run solved Phase 4's 27 households that save into a pension or an
ISA twice, with and without the split (CANDS, CANDD), on today's candidate, on the same 4,000 simulated markets. Every
household took the candidate's own menu, the solve on anchors. On anchors the split's copies are not skipped exactly
outside the working years, so CANDS also differs a little from CANDD in other years (`B7-FACTORIALS.md`).

**What it found:**
- **Survival rises on the panel and on 14 households, beyond noise; it falls beyond noise on none.**
  - The panel, each household counted once: `+0.398` points (se `0.0270`).
  - Each of the 14 beyond noise after Holm (`results-save-beside.txt`, section 1), the largest S326F `+1.225` (50 saved,
    1 lost of 4,000), S272 `+0.975` and S260 `+0.825`.
- **Spending moves beyond noise both ways.** By the rule's verdict (`b7-verdict.mjs`), **A GAIN AND A HARM**:
  - **the gains:** 23 households spend more, each beyond noise after Holm. The panel, each household counted once,
    `+0.4233%` (`+0.4129` to `+0.4336`); pooled in pounds `+0.3942%`;
  - **the harm:** S308F spends `0.250%` less over its lifetime (se `0.0562`; Holm `1.35e-3`). Its survival is level,
    `-0.050` (30 saved, 32 lost). Nothing here shows why: no decomposition was run.
  - Without S308F, the panel's spending is `+0.4491%`.
- **Where the split put the saving** (section 4, from the policy beside the engine):
  - the pension first on 90% or more of the decisions on 12 households;
  - the ISA first almost always on five of the younger cohort, who retire at 52, before their pensions open: S304F,
    S306F, S308F, S310F and S340F. S308F, the harmed household, is one of them, at 99%;
  - a mix on the other ten.
- **The estate** is lower on the panel by `£109,456` a path (se `28,198`), and moves both ways and widely by household:
  `-£5,934,154` a path on S334F and `+£3,094,369` on S310F.
- **The checks:** the gate fair (one code and audit stamp, every household's two arms on one plan, one menu, anchors on
  all 27); `runPolicy` beside the engine on 54,000 paths, none differing.

## What it leads to (B7)

- **The harm** (S308F's spending) goes to the maintainer at once, with every effect beside it.
- **The gains** go to the maintainer as a proposal for a registered test (v2), with every effect beside it. They are
  survival on 14 households and spending on 23, with the panel up on both, and are larger than any B7 read so far. Nothing
  changes in the candidate or the app on any outcome.
- **Two things stand between these gains and a v2 test:**
  - **The menu.** On anchors the split's copies are not skipped exactly outside the working years, so the gains are not
    shown to be the split's own. A v2 runs on the whole menu, or measures the skip's part beside it (the windfall read's
    reviewer, second round, MINOR 2).
  - **The engine's ISA defect** (`reports/2026-11-18-windfall-read.md`). It reaches this panel on one household:
    - S310F, whose own policy puts its windfall into its ISA in a retired year in both arms;
    - the split's own ISA route goes through the year's regular contributions, which are counted
      (`results-save-isa-deposits.txt`);
    - its effect here is unmeasured.
- **The agent's recommendation:** a v2 test of the savings split, after the defect is fixed, on the whole menu or with the
  skip's part measured. The harm on S308F should be looked at in it: one of the five households saving ISA first, its
  survival level and its spending down a quarter of a percent.
