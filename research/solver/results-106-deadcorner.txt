The size miss is expected and not a problem for the conclusion: the ~30% was a one-read estimate that
held the other corners fixed, while the clamp also enters every backward step, so the whole table
shifts. What matters is that the clamp - the mechanism's own dial - moves S126 by 38 points and moves
five controls by at most half a point. Nothing else in the solver distinguishes S126 from S184, S240,
S300 and S004, which share its pension share; only the dead node does.

DOES IT REACH DECISIONS? Yes, at least in how moves are scored. The solver's own score for its best
opening move reads 4.1% survival at today's clamp (35.9% at 1e-3), against 96.8% simulated. Rankings can
survive a biased level, so this does not prove a wrong choice; the circumstantial evidence that it does
is in 6f: with resilience off, S126 is the only one of six households that never fully funds, and its
1.6 trimmed years match its two-year bridge, with 4.9 years of liquid money available. That is the
predicted signature - extra trimming in bridge years - and it is the user's trust requirement.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE REST OF THE PLAN
- Published survival numbers are unaffected: every result is scored by simulation, not by the table.
- The fault is on a SHARE axis, which Phase V as designed never varied. Per the amendment written
  before this run, V3 extends to reads between share nodes and V2 gains a share-axis refinement BEFORE
  Phase V runs.
- The fix is NOT made here. It lands once, after Phase V, so a second interpolation fault is fixed in
  the same change, with one field check, before 6d stage 2 and Phase 4. Candidate fixes, to be chosen
  by Phase V's measurements rather than now: (a) read survival linearly when any corner is at the clamp
  (the cliff the log-odds were chosen for runs along total wealth, not the shares); (b) a share node at
  each household's own opening share; (c) mark cells that cannot fund the bridge and exclude them from
  the read.
- How common is it in real use? Retired before pension access with more than 80% in the pension is a
  common UK shape (a DC-heavy early retiree). One of 41 in the library; plausibly more among real users.
