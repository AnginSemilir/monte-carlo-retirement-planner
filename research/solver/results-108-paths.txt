----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE MECHANISM IS VISIBLE IN THE SOLVE COUNTS, AND IT IS NOT "MORE NOISE, SLIGHTLY WORSE ANSWER"

Read the solves column. At 600 and 1,200 paths, households land in ONE solve. One solve means the very
first test - `verify(rHi).floorRate >= confidence` at the top of the bracket, where NO trimming happens -
came back true. The noisy estimate told the solver the untrimmed plan already clears the ask, so it did
nothing and stopped. Then the held-out draw says otherwise.

So the failure is not a slightly mis-tuned dial. **It is the search never starting.** S020, S268 and
S342 each collapse from six or seven solves to one as the sample shrinks.

**AND THE FAILURES ARE FAST, WHICH IS THE TRAP.** A one-solve collapse costs 338-836 s against
3,296-7,159 s for a real landing. A study that compared path counts on SPEED would have reported a
four-to-eight-fold saving and recommended 1,200. The saving is entirely the solver giving up early on
a wrong reading. This reducer scores the promise first and the clock second, which is the only ordering
that could have caught it.

S154 is the sharpest illustration: at 2,400 it lands in ONE solve at +0.73, closer to its ask than the
six-solve answer at 5,400 (+1.03). The worse method got the better-looking number, by luck.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT SETTLES, AND WHAT IT REFUSES TO SETTLE

**Settled: 5,400 is load-bearing.** It was an unexamined constant; it is now a measured floor with the
failures below it documented. That was worth the six hours on its own, and it closes the largest
remaining "nobody ever checked this" item in the speed programme.

**Refused: this does NOT say 5,400 is optimal**, only that 600, 1,200 and 2,400 are not enough. The
count between 2,400 and 5,400 is unexplored, and there is no reason to think the boundary is exactly
where it was first guessed. It was not worth another six hours to find out, because the prize - some
fraction of the gap between 2,400 and 5,400 - is small beside what the same hours buy elsewhere.

**The real cost of a landing, measured:** 1,599 to 7,159 s at 5,400 paths, mean 4,709 s. That is the
number Phase 4 should be sized from - roughly 13 hours on four cores for its solver arm alone.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AND IT HANDED OVER A FINDING IT WAS NOT LOOKING FOR

Four landings per household, at four path counts, is a coarse (lambda, floor rate) curve - free, as a
by-product. Read that way it answered a question a separate 1.6-hour run had been queued for: the floor
rate is MONOTONE in lambda, 0 reversals in 15 adjacent pairs. That run was cancelled before starting.

More importantly, the 5,400 column shows **every household overshooting its ask**, by +0.37 to +2.73,
with the biggest overshoots on the households that used their full seven-solve budget. That is the
signature of a search that stopped before converging, and it is what the convergence test now running
was built to confirm. See results-converge.txt when it lands.
