The floor landing: why it missed, what fixed it, and what over-fixing it cost

THE SYMPTOM. In the flex-mix pilot (three-world mixture, CONF=gkFloor, margin 0.5pt), the solver met
its floor promise on 21 of the first 24 households. Three fell short by about a point:

  id     ask    held-out floor   short
  S004   92.2       91.17        -1.03
  S178   86.1       85.13        -0.97
  S184   74.6       73.40        -1.20

A fourth, S162, was labelled "confidence not reachable" after 2 solves but is not a failure: its
held-out floor was 98.00 against an ask of 98.07, inside the reducer's own criterion. Its search-path
rate at the bracket bottom read 98.50 against a target of 98.60, so it missed by a tenth of a point on
a noisy estimate and aborted. Same cause, different symptom.

THE CAUSE. Not the solver. The estimator. Every one of the three MET its target on the paths lambda
was chosen on, and only fell short on the paths it was judged on:

  id     ask+margin   floor on search paths   floor on held-out   gap    SE of a 600-path estimate
  S004      92.7            93.17                  91.17        +2.00            1.16
  S178      86.6            86.67                  85.13        +1.54            1.45
  S184      75.1            75.50                  73.40        +2.10            1.80
  S162      98.6            98.50                  98.00        +0.50            0.57

Every gap is about one standard error of the estimate the bisection was steering by. solveFlex chose
lambda on 600 paths from seed 7001 while the ask and the verdict came from 3,000 paths of seed 7002.
That is the arrangement optimizeSpend in App.jsx already warns about, in a comment written after the
same bug was found there: "searching and reporting on different samples is how a solver quietly lies...
all twelve of twelve test fixtures came back 0.5 to 2.4 points BELOW the target the user had asked
for." Our 1.5 to 2.1 sits inside that range.

Two effects compound. The small sample reads high, and then choosing the largest lambda that still
clears on it adds selection bias on top - the winner's curse. The low bisection counts (4 to 6 solves)
were a symptom rather than a separate problem: the loop breaks as soon as the noisy estimate clears
the target, so noise ends the search early.

SEPARATING THE TWO (research/solver/seedcheck.mjs). Running the SAME fixed policy, with no lambda
selection involved, across five path samples:

  id     7001/600   7002/3000   7001/3000   9001/3000   9002/3000
  S004     93.83      92.23       93.13       92.97       91.17
  S172     95.33      94.93       94.73       94.50       94.50
  S194     91.17      89.07       90.10       90.47       89.60
  S162     98.67      98.07       97.83       97.70       97.87

The 600-path prefix reads highest in all four. CAVEAT, and it matters: pathsForSeed is deterministic
per index, so the same seed gives the same draws to every household. These four rows are one
favourable sample observed four times, not four independent observations. The direction is
suggestive; the confirmation below is the evidence.

CONFIRMATION BY BRUTE FORCE. Same code, search paths 600 -> 5,400, on the three that missed:

  id     ask    before   after    was      now     time before   time after   solves
  S004   92.2   91.17    93.00   -1.03   +0.80        749s         2093s       4 -> 7
  S178   86.1   85.13    86.83   -0.97   +0.73       1344s         1893s       6 -> 7
  S184   74.6   73.40    74.73   -1.20   +0.13       1219s         2814s       5 -> 7

All three land. Cost 2.2x on average, from both the extra paths and the extra solves the better
estimate justifies. An earlier claim that the paths were nearly free was wrong: it was measured on a
single-table fixed policy, and a forward run under the mixture consults three tables through
chooseAction.

THE FIX, AND THE WORSE BUG IT FIRST INTRODUCED. solveFlex now draws one sample, so the search set is a
genuine prefix of the verification set rather than a different draw, and re-measures the chosen table
on the full draw before returning. What is returned in floorRate is what was checked.

The first version of that verification walked lambda down by bisecting between the chosen value and the
bracket bottom, accepting the first lambda that cleared. The bottom sits ten to twenty times below the
answer, so one step overshot, every time:

  id     ask    floor reached   over    lambda (was)      never below target   years at target (was)
  S004   92.2       94.30      +2.10   0.0431 (0.4472)          0.0%             0.61 (1.00)
  S178   86.1       89.50      +3.40   0.0357 (0.3075)          0.0%             0.45 (1.00)
  S184   74.6       81.93      +7.33   0.0626 (0.9457)          6.1%             0.53 (1.00)

Every lambda collapsed into a narrow band regardless of where it started, which is the bracket bottom
dominating rather than the household's own answer, and the overshoot grows with how far the true
lambda sat above the bottom. All three landed their floor and all three gave up roughly half their
years at the full target to do it. A floor exists to protect spending; clearing it by two to seven
points of headroom spends exactly what it was protecting. That is a worse bug than the undershoot.

The unit suite passed both the broken version and the repair, because it cannot see over-trimming.
A landing assertion with teeth is owed: the verified floor must not exceed the ask by more than a small
allowance unless lambda sits at the bracket bottom, tested on a household whose lambda lands high.

Stage 2 now interpolates for the crossing instead of bisecting to it. The floor rate is smooth and
monotone in log lambda, so the straight line through the two verified points lands near the answer in
about one solve where bisection from the bottom needed four or five (regula falsi, the idiom App.jsx
already uses on the sacrifice curve). solver-flex passes at 17 with lambda 0.245 in 9 solves. The
field check on S004 and S184 was still running when this was written.

WHAT IS PROVEN AND WHAT IS NOT. Proven: single-stage on 5,400 paths lands all three, at 2.2x the cost.
Not yet proven: the two-stage design is cheaper for the same landing. It must beat the single stage
head to head on the same households, on landing, on years at target and on time, before it is the
default. Setting verifyPaths equal to searchPaths degrades the new code to exactly the proven single
stage, so that fallback is one option away.

THE SAME PATTERN ELSEWHERE, audited.

  site                                    search   reported on          verified?
  optimizeSpend (safe spend)                 400        5,000           yes, one seed + walk-down
  safeRetirementAge                          400        5,000           yes, one seed + walk later
  Monte Carlo tab spend dial                 400        5,000           yes, one seed + confirm loop
  solveFlex (this)                           600        3,000 (other)   no -> fixed here
  tournament strategy pick                   400        re-scored 4,000 headline yes; panel no
  pickFixed (experiment opponent)            600        3,000 (other)   no, and defensible

The tournament's headline figures were already honest: every player is re-scored at TOURNAMENT_TRIALS
after the search and the ranking uses those. Only the collapsed per-candidate panel showed the
400-path estimates, and it now states the path count and says the winner's figure there reads high.

pickFixed is left alone on purpose. Choosing the opponent on search data and scoring it on held-out
data models what the app actually does for a user, and both sides are chosen the same way, so the
comparison stays fair. The asymmetry was only ever in the promise: a claimed floor rate has to be
verified on the sample it is claimed about.

CONTAMINATION, recorded because it is the reason the pilot is not reportable. solve.js was edited three
times while the 41-household flex-mix run was in flight, and the batch spawns a fresh Node per
household, so households that started after each edit read different code. The 33 that finished before
the first edit are clean and carry no verifyPaths field; the rest are a mix. Experiments must run from
a snapshot of the tree, and every result record should carry a solver version stamp so this is visible
in the JSON rather than reconstructed from process start times.
