AUDIT: FLAT REGIONS AND UNJUSTIFIED CONSTANTS IN THE SOLVER
====================================================================================================

Asked for after the bequest cap turned out to hide GBP 12.8M of pot movement on the clean 41: go
looking for the same shape elsewhere. A "flat region" here means a place where more of something the
household actually cares about earns the objective NOTHING, so the solver cannot see it and will not
trade for it. A "cliff" is the same fault's twin: a place where one pound changes the answer by a step.

Nine things checked. Two are clean, two are dormant, two need a sweep that has never been run, and
three are live faults with the field numbers below. Measured on the clean 41 unless stated; the two
probes are research/solver/audit-pcls.mjs and audit-gain.mjs, both walking each household's OWN model
forward on its own plan, no solver involved.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LIVE 1. THE UNREALISED-GAIN AXIS TOPS OUT AT 55%, AND HOUSEHOLDS REACH 89%           (the worst one)

  grid.js:104   const gain = opts.gainBuckets || [0.05, 0.25, 0.55];
  grid.js:190   const ig = nearest(g.gain, s[3]);        <- nearest, NOT interpolated
  grid.js:279   interp() is trilinear over the three pots AT the nearest gain and pcls buckets

  The fraction of a general investment account that is unrealised gain decides its CGT bill. It is a
  grid dimension with three representative values and no interpolation, so the boundaries sit at 15%
  and 40%, and above 55% the axis is FLAT.

  Measured, 41 households, every year each one holds a GIA:

      household-years with a GIA        1,485 of 1,781
      snapped more than 5 points away   1,035 of 1,485   (70%)
      worst snap                        34.0 points      (S300, year 60: true 89.0%, read as 55%)
      highest gain fraction reached     89.0%            top bucket is 55%

      id     peak gain   read as
      S300      89.0%      55%          S268      82.4%      55%
      S240      84.8%      55%          S390      80.3%      55%
      S252      84.8%      55%          S410      79.0%      55%
      S276      84.8%      55%          S292      78.9%      55%
      S330      83.7%      55%          S414      78.9%      55%

  Direction: a cell at the 55% bucket was built with a GIA whose cost base is 45% of its value, so a
  household at 89% is valued as though liquidating its GIA cost 55%-of-value in taxable gain when it
  really costs 89%. The solver OVER-VALUES those positions and under-prices the CGT of touching them.
  CGT is on by default (App.jsx:493 cgtEnabled: true), so this is live for every household with a GIA.

  This is the bequest cap's shape exactly: a ceiling chosen as "representative", and the field runs a
  long way past it.

LIVE 3. TWO DEFINITIONS OF "WHAT IS LEFT", ON ADJACENT LINES

  alive = !(floor > 0 && total < floor)      <- config.solvencyFloor judged on the GROSS pot
  b += WEIGHTS[zi] * (alive ? beqOf(net) : 0) <- the estate valued NET of pension death tax

  The minimum-pot promise is kept in gross pounds and the bequest is counted in net ones. Dormant in
  practice: all 210 library households run at pensionDeathTaxRate = 0, so gross == net and no run has
  ever exercised the difference. It becomes live the moment a user sets a death-tax rate, which the UI
  allows. Recorded, not fixed: fixing it without a household that shows the difference would be tuning
  against nothing. solver-minpot.test.mjs A2 sets 40% inside the test for exactly this reason.

DORMANT 1. THE RAISE CREDIT IS CAPPED EXACTLY AT THE TOP OF THE SHIPPED MENU

  solve.js:312   const creditOf = (level) => Math.sqrt(Math.min(0.2, level - 1));

  Credit for spending ABOVE target stops at level 1.2. Above it the marginal credit is exactly zero:
  a household spending 30% or 50% over target earns no more than one spending 20% over. The shipped
  menu is LEVELS=1.2,1.1,1,0.9,0.8, which tops out at precisely 1.2, so the cap never binds today.
  It becomes a flat region the first time anyone adds a 1.3 level - the same shape as the bequest cap,
  waiting. Worth a comment in the code saying the two numbers are tied.

DORMANT 2. THE BEQUEST CAP'S OTHER TWO UNJUSTIFIED PROPERTIES

  Beyond the flat top already recorded in Phase 6c: the cap is 4 x opening wealth, a FIXED multiple
  against a quantity that depends on the horizon, and it is measured at PLAN time rather than at
  retirement. S342 is 37 with 28 working years to go, so its cap is set against a balance it will not
  have for nearly three decades. Neither property was chosen; both were inherited.

NEVER SWEPT (a family exists, one member was tried)

  shortExp = 2      the trim penalty's exponent, lambda * sum (1 - level)^shortExp. Five mentions in
                    PLAN.md, no results file studies it. The exponent decides whether the solver would
                    rather take one big cut or several small ones, which is a question about the
                    household's taste, not about arithmetic.
  wR = 0.5          the resilience weight. Touched once in results-2c3-tuning.txt, never swept since
                    the objective took its current form.
  SWITCH_COST 0.0025 / SWITCH_MARGIN 0.001   the cost of a tier change and the gain one must beat.
                    No results file mentions either. Both were set by argument, not measurement.

CLEAN (checked and found sound)

  resilK = scale    The shortfall measure 1 - min(1, max(0, resilK - net) / resilK) saturates at 1
                    once the terminal pot clears opening wealth, which looks like a flat top. It is
                    not one in the field: 0 of 41 households have their unlucky tenth (p10 terminal
                    net) clear opening wealth, so the term discriminates everywhere it is used.
                    Saturation at the top is also what a DOWNSIDE measure is supposed to do.

  grid top          max(60 years of spend, 6 x opening wealth in years of spend) per axis. Generous
                    on purpose and measured in results-grid-ceiling.txt. The top is not a flat region;
                    it is a boundary, and the study showed nothing lives against it.

  lambda bracket    2.0 down to 0.005. Seven of 41 sat at the top in gate 6b, which is not a failure:
                    at the top the solver chose NO trimming and the de-risking alone met the ask. The
                    bracket end is a label, and solve.js says so. Already on the record in 6b.

WHAT THIS CHANGES

  Nothing yet. None of these is fixed here, because the standing rule is to record what is measured
  and change one thing at a time with a field check. The three live ones want, in order of size:

    1. the gain axis:  interpolate it instead of snapping, or raise the top bucket past 0.85. The
       first costs one more interpolated dimension (2x the reads on that axis); the second is free.
    2. the pcls axis:  same choice, plus deriving the lump-taken flag from the true state rather than
       from the bucket, which is free and is a correctness fix, not a resolution one.
    3. gross/net:      one line, but no household in the library can show the difference. Needs a
       fixture with a death-tax rate before it is worth touching.

  Written up before any of them is attempted, so the field numbers above are the before.
