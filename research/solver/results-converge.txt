THE CONVERGENCE TEST: MY HYPOTHESIS WAS WRONG, AND THE REAL CAUSE IS BETTER NEWS AND WORSE
====================================================================================================

Run 23 Sep 09:15-11:16 from snapshot /tmp/solver-snap-cYIajz (commit 70a3987). Four households landed
at `bisectSteps = 12` against their recorded five-step results. Prediction written before the run, in
batch-converge.sh.

    id      ask     margin 5 -> 12      lambda 5 -> 12       spend level 5 -> 12    solves
    S268   75.1    +2.73 -> +1.90    0.9457 -> 1.0385      1.0676 -> 1.0700       7 -> 8
    S292   77.2    +2.37 -> +2.37    unchanged             unchanged              7 -> 7
    S342   72.0    +1.87 -> +1.87    unchanged             unchanged              7 -> 7
    S058   97.9    +0.37 -> +0.37    unchanged             unchanged              3 -> 3   (control)

PREDICTED: S268 +2.73 -> ~1.6, S292 +2.37 -> ~1.3, S342 +1.87 -> ~0.8, control unchanged.
HAPPENED: one of three moved, roughly as predicted. TWO DID NOT MOVE AT ALL. Control unchanged.

**THE HYPOTHESIS IS FALSIFIED ON TWO OF THREE.** The bracket width is not the binding constraint.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS ACTUALLY STOPPING THE SEARCH, READ OFF THE SOLVE COUNTS

Solves = 2 bracket ends + loop iterations. With `bisectSteps = 12` the loop COULD run twelve times.

    S268   8 solves = 2 + 6 iterations, then stopped
    S292   7 solves = 2 + 5, then stopped
    S342   7 solves = 2 + 5, then stopped

**They stopped with six or seven iterations of budget unused. The tolerance fired.** And at five steps
S292 and S342 also used 2 + 5 - so the tolerance was satisfiable at exactly the same iteration, and the
extra budget had nothing to do. S268 was the one household whose sixth iteration mattered, and it is
worth **0.83 of a point** and 0.0024 of spending level.

**So the over-trimming is real - all 22 landings do overshoot - but the BRACKET is not why.** The
stopping rule is:

    break when  SEARCH rate <= ask + margin(0.5) + tol(0.5)  =  ask + 1.0

and the reported margin is measured on HELD-OUT paths, a different draw. The solver stops when its
SEARCH sample says it is within a point of the ask, and the held-out truth then comes in at +1.9 to
+2.4. **The search and held-out estimates disagree by about 1.4 points on these three, all in the same
direction.**

That direction is the surprise. The winner's curse predicts held-out LOWER than search, and the half-
point margin exists to absorb exactly that. Here held-out is HIGHER. On three households it is not a
conclusion, but it is the first evidence that **the two seeds do not agree as closely as the design
assumes**, which is the "one seed pair throughout" item already recorded as unresolved.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS CHANGES

**The fix is NOT more steps.** Twelve steps bought 0.83 of a point on one household of three and
nothing on the rest, for double the solves. Raising `bisectSteps` is close to worthless.

**The fix IS the stopping rule**, which is what the sensitivity analysis had already argued from the
78-fold spread in how much the floor rate moves per unit of lambda. That argument now has a run behind
it rather than only algebra. Stop on ESTIMATED REMAINING ERROR - slope x ln(bracket), with the slope
read from pairs the search has already computed - rather than on whether a sample estimate has landed
inside a fixed window.

**And Phase 4's fairness worry shrinks.** The concern was a one-sided handicap against the solver on
spending delivered, because the app's own optimizeSpend converges to a 250-pound bracket while ours
stops early. Since more steps change almost nothing, that handicap is **smaller than feared** - worth
fixing, no longer a precondition.

**What does not change: the overshoot itself.** Every landing still delivers more safety than asked and
pays for it in spending. The cause is now located in the stopping rule and in the search/held-out gap,
not in the bracket, and the remedy is different from the one proposed yesterday.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PROCESS NOTE, WHICH IS THE POINT OF WRITING PREDICTIONS DOWN

The prediction was specific enough to be wrong in a useful way. Had it said only "more steps should
help", one household improving would have looked like confirmation. Because it named which households
should move and by how much, two of them not moving AT ALL was unmissable, and the solve counts then
said why within minutes.

The first version of this finding also claimed the bracket was a factor of 12.5 when it is 1.21 -
corrected before the run. Had that error survived, the run would have been read as "the fix works
weakly" instead of "the diagnosis was wrong".

====================================================================================================
CORRECTION, SAME DAY: THE "HELD-OUT RUNS HIGHER" CLAIM ABOVE WAS A SELECTION EFFECT

Checked against every flex-tiers household, same policy scored on both draws (search: seed 7001, 5,400
paths; held-out: seed 7002, 3,000 paths):

    held-out higher on        26 of 41, not all
    mean gap (held - search)  +0.18 pts, median +0.08, range -0.47 to +1.64
    per-household noise       +/-0.91 pts

**+0.18 is noise.** The ~1.4-point gap reported above came from S268, S292 and S342 - which were chosen
FOR THIS TEST because they had the largest overshoots. A household picked for looking extreme on one
draw looks extreme partly by luck, and will regress on another. **I selected on the outcome.** The
claim is withdrawn, and the "one seed pair" item stays where it was: unresolved, not evidenced.

WHAT THE OVERSHOOT ACTUALLY IS, averaged over the 22 genuine landings:

    deliberate margin                   +0.50   set on purpose against the winner's curse
    tolerance window                   ~+0.33   the search stops anywhere in +0.5..+1.0 above the ask
    draw-to-draw noise                 ~+0.33   zero in expectation, positive in this sample
    held-out total                      +1.16
    (search-draw margin                 +0.83   squarely inside the window the stopping rule defines)

**So there is no correctness fault.** The search lands exactly where its settings put it. What remains
is one design choice - the half-point tolerance window buys up to half a point of extra safety with
spending, and narrowing it costs solves - and one real consequence for Phase 4, recorded there: the
two arms do not land to the same effective target.

PROCESS: a test's households must not be chosen by the outcome the test is meant to explain. A
representative set with a control would have avoided a false finding that survived an hour.

CLOSED ACROSS ALL 22, FROM EXISTING RECORDS (no new run)

The stopping rule lands a household between +0.5 and +1.0 above its ask ON THE SEARCH DRAW. Above +1.0
means the budget ran out before the tolerance fired - the only case more steps can help. flex-tiers
records the search-draw rate for every landing:

    inside the window (tolerance fired)     19 of 22
    above +1.0 (budget-limited)              3 of 22   S020 +1.12, S268 +1.40, S354 +1.01
    below +0.5 (impossible by construction)  0

**S268 is the one household that moved in the convergence test; S292 and S342, which did not, are not
on the budget-limited list.** The existing records would have predicted the test's outcome had they been
read first. More steps can help at most 3 of 22 households, by under a point. Closed.

THE HALF-POINT MARGIN IS ADEQUATE AND SHOULD NOT BE CUT. It absorbs the held-out draw coming in below the
search draw: across 41 households that happened on 14, worst -0.47 (S400), and no genuine landing fell
below its ask on held-out (minimum +0.37). The margin covers the worst case using 0.47 of its 0.5 - it
is load-bearing. Of the ~1 point of extra safety, the MARGIN is load-bearing and the TOLERANCE WINDOW is
discretionary; if either is ever trimmed, it is the window.
