WHAT THIS SETTLES

The unrealised-gain axis tops out at 55% while households reach 89%, and 70% of GIA-holding
household-years snap more than five points from the truth. Both facts stand - they were measured. What
the screen adds is that **neither changes the answer**: moving the ceiling to 0.80 costs at most 1.74%
of median pot, and removing the snap entirely costs at most 0.22%.

So the buckets [0.05, 0.25, 0.55] now stand on evidence rather than on nobody having looked. That is a
different and better position than the one the audit found them in, and it is the whole return on the
2.2 hours.

**Consequences, all of them subtractive, which is the point of a screen:**
  - 6e stage 2 (~13 h wall) does not run.
  - E3 and E4 are sized against a grid that is not going to grow: no fourth gain bucket, so E3's
    30.2% is not cancelled by a 33% cell increase.

    owed: no fresh 6c run, because the fix that would have triggered it is not happening.
  - The audit's findings 1 and 2 close as recorded-and-checked rather than recorded-and-pending.

THE ONE RESULT I DID NOT TAKE AT FACE VALUE

The lump-flag arm returned +0.00% on all twelve, to the pound. An arm that changes nothing on every
household is as likely to be an arm that never applied - two tests earlier this week were vacuous in
exactly that way - so it was checked rather than accepted.

  - the knobs recorded in every record say `pclsStrict: true`, so the flag was set;
  - walked forward, S172 spends 38 of its 40 years with between 0 and 25% of its allowance used, and
    the strict rule reads a DIFFERENT CELL in all 38. S410: 38 of 46, likewise. **The arm bit.**
  - and the value at those cells is identical to four decimal places on survival and to the pound on
    bequest. The read moved; the answer did not.

That raised a bigger question - if the lump-sum axis makes no difference, it is a whole grid dimension
tripling the cell count for nothing, which would dwarf E3 and E4 together. **Tested, and the answer is
no.** Reading one table at the same position across its three buckets, over every cell and year:

    id     worst survival spread    worst bequest spread    cells where the chosen MOVE differs
    S172        5.840 pts                  7.178%            5,247 of 86,400   (6.07%)
    S184        5.522 pts                  7.948%            5,472 of 88,560   (6.18%)
    S300      100.000 pts                 11.477%            6,116 of 131,760  (4.64%)
    S390       14.194 pts                  4.666%            5,618 of 99,360   (5.65%)

The axis carries real information; S172's and S410's own positions simply sit where two of its buckets
agree. **The idea of collapsing it is dead, killed on evidence, and recorded here so nobody has it
again.**

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE LIMITATION, STATED RATHER THAN BURIED

**The twelve were chosen as worst on the GAIN axis, not the lump-sum axis.** Only two of the eight
households the audit found sitting in lump-sum bucket 0 - S410 and S172 - are in this sample. So the
gain arms were tested where they bite hardest, and the flag arm was not.

The pre-registered gate was written over these twelve and its verdict stands as written. But the
honest summary of the flag arm is **"no effect on two households chosen for something else"**, not
"no effect". The remaining six - S374, S194, S206, S230, S218, S112 - are six cells, about seventeen
minutes, and are run as a follow-up rather than claiming what has not been measured.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT CANNOT SAY

Lambda was HELD at each household's landed flex-tiers value, so the floor rate is not pinned, the arms
are not compared at equal downside, and no figure here is a headline. It answers "does the ceiling
matter", not "how much better is the solver".

A cosmetic fault in the running snapshot: under SOLVERONLY the progress line read rival arms that were
no longer being scored, so every cell wrote its record and then died on the console print. The records
are complete - the write happens first - and the working tree was fixed rather than restarting 2.2
hours of work for a print statement.

THE FOLLOW-UP: THE FLAG ARM WHERE THE FAULT IS LARGEST                       (run 23 Sep, 12 cells)

The other six households the audit found in lump-sum bucket 0, each read by the shipped grid as having
taken no lump sum at all:

    id      allowance used    median pot (cur)    with the flag fix      change
    S374         7.1%             315,891             315,891           +0.000%
    S194         8.8%             302,287             302,287           +0.000%
    S206        10.2%             579,600             579,600           +0.000%
    S230        11.6%             616,035             616,035           +0.000%
    S218        16.8%             327,354             327,354           +0.000%
    S112        19.1%             991,226             991,226           +0.000%

Identical to the pound, and no floor rate moved. **All eight affected households are now tested - two
in stage 1, six here - and the flag fix changes nothing on any of them.**

**DECISION: record it, do NOT change the code.** That is the opposite of what I expected to conclude,
and the reason is worth stating because it is not laziness.

`pclsStrict` was described as a correctness fix, and half of it is: under the shipped rule a household
that has spent part of its tax-free lump is valued as one that may still take the whole thing, which is
an action it does not have. But look at what the fix substitutes. S374 has used 7.1% of its allowance.
The shipped rule reads that as 0%; the "fix" reads it as 50%. **Neither is right.** The fix trades a
wrong FLAG for a wrong FIGURE - correct about whether the lump has gone, and further from the truth
about how much allowance remains.

So it is not a fix, it is a different approximation, and the field says both approximations are
invisible: zero change on eight of eight households where it can possibly bite, and provably no change
at all on the other thirty-three, because the two rules differ only when the allowance used is between
zero and a quarter of the LSA.

Changing working code for no measured benefit, where the change is itself a trade rather than a
correction, is how a codebase acquires risk without acquiring anything else. The shipped rule stays.
Audit finding 2 closes as **measured, understood, and deliberately not acted on** - which is a stronger
place to leave it than an unexamined fault or an unjustified patch.

`pclsStrict` stays in the code as an option, defaulted off, with its gate. It cost nothing to keep and
the next person to notice the flag can run the arm in half an hour instead of rediscovering it.
