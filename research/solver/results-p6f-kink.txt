At today's weight, S390 spends 14.6 of its ~45 years below target and S112 13.8 of 35, against 2.8
and 4.0 without the term, while the floor rate differs by 0.4 to 2.1 points. Fully-funded rate at
wR = 0 against 0.5: S390 69% -> 0%, S112 56% -> 0%, S206 79% -> 36%. **Resilience is the main driver
of trimming in this solver.** The unlucky tenth's END POT is protected by cutting spending during LIFE.

CAVEAT that bounds this: lambda is held, so at wR = 0 the floor rate sits 0.3 to 2.1 points lower.
Re-landing wR = 0 to the same ask would put some trimming back. The screen cannot say how much; the
gap in trimming (roughly 3x to 5x) is much larger than a 0.3-2.1 point floor-rate gap usually costs,
so the direction is not in doubt, the size is.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR THE PRODUCT. The fixed requirements say the user chooses what they value -
survival alone, or also a minimum end-of-life pot, or also the median pot - and "needs to trust that
the trimming of retirement spending is minimised". Resilience is none of those levers. It is the
solver's own preference for a fat unlucky-tenth estate, it is on for every user, and it is the largest
source of trimming. The user already has a lever that asks for exactly this, explicitly: the minimum
end-of-life pot. So the re-anchoring design has a real option the plan did not list: **wR = 0 by
default, with downside wealth protected only as far as the user asks through their own pot floor.**
That is a product decision for the maintainer, not a tuning result.

the six that still never fully funds (0%), and its 1.6 trimmed years match its two-year bridge, with
4.9 years of liquid money available to fund it. That is what the dead-corner hypothesis predicts. The
#106 run (started 12:49) tests the mechanism directly.
