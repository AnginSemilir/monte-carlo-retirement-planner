PART E, MEASURED: E4 IS DEAD AND THE SOLVE IS THE MINORITY OF A LANDING
====================================================================================================

Two twenty-minute measurements, run 22-23 Sep on a quiet box, both of which change what Part E should
do. Neither is an argument; both are stopwatches, because three cost estimates this week were wrong by
factors of three to twenty and every one of them came from reasoning rather than timing.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E4: DEAD. INTERLEAVING IS SLOWER, NOT FASTER.

The proposal: `readValues` reads four separate arrays at the same index - survival, bequest, resilience,
shortfall - so eight corners touch up to 32 cache lines. At 30 points each array is 76 KB and the four
are 304 KB, well past a 32-48 KB L1. Store the four values adjacently and a corner becomes 32 bytes,
one line: 8 lines a call instead of 32.

Measured at the real shape (9,720 cells, readValues' own eight-corner pattern, starting cell drawn
fresh each call, best of seven alternating rounds, both layouts asserted to produce identical totals):

    four separate arrays     18.9 ms    47.1 ns a call
    one interleaved array    19.6 ms    48.9 ns a call
    speedup                  0.963x

**Interleaving is 3.8% SLOWER.** The prefetcher handles four sequential streams at least as well as one
strided one, and the strided access defeats it. The plan said "cache guesses are exactly the guesses
that come out wrong" and told this phase to measure before building. It did, and the guess was wrong.

**E4's build is cancelled.** Twenty minutes rather than a morning, which is what the instruction was for.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SPLIT: SOLVING IS 45.7% OF A LANDING. PART E ONLY TOUCHES THAT HALF.

Timers inside `solveFlex`, around the solve and around `rateOn`, off without SOLVER_PROFILE. S184 at
the real configuration - 30 points, 5,400 search paths, three-world mixture, tiers on:

    solving (2 tables)                628.8 s    45.7%
    running paths forward (10,800)    746.6 s    54.3%
    landing, total                   1375.4 s

    per solve        314.4 s
    per 1,000 paths   69.1 s

**The ratio is robust to how many solves a landing takes**, because each solve is followed by exactly
one pass over the search paths. At two solves or at eight, it is the same 46/54. This household landed
at the bracket floor in two solves; an eight-solve landing is 2,515 s of solving against 2,985 s of
paths, the same split.

**So every phase of Part E - E0, E1, E2, E3, E4 - speeds up 45.7% of a landing and leaves 54.3%
untouched.** E3's measured 30.2% of CELL work is therefore **13.8% of a landing**, for a day and a half
of building. That is not nothing, and E3 is exact and safe. But it is not what it looked like.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS PROMOTES: THE SEARCH-PATH COUNT (task #108), NEVER SWEPT

Every solve is followed by 5,400 forward runs. At 69.1 s per thousand that is 373 s a solve - MORE than
the 314 s the solve itself costs. The number was chosen once and has never been swept.

If 1,000 paths landed as well as 5,400, the forward half shrinks by about 81%, which is **roughly 44%
off a landing** - more than three times E3's 13.8%, and it is a parameter sweep rather than new code.

**It is not free money, and the reason is in solve.js already.** The search paths are not only used to
MEASURE a policy, they are used to CHOOSE lambda. With a small sample the bisection picks whichever
lambda that sample flattered - the winner's curse - and 2d finding (d) measured the damage: landings
undershooting their ask by up to 0.8 of a point on held-out paths. Cutting the sample attacks precisely
the property the landing exists to deliver.

So #108 is a real trade-off study with a real chance of concluding "5,400 is needed". That is worth
knowing either way, and it is worth knowing BEFORE a day and a half goes into E3, because every field
check from here pays the 5,400 and none of them pays E3 until it is built.

RECOMMENDATION, and the reordering it implies:
  1. **#108 first.** Sweep the landing against search-path count. Cheap to run, no new code, and it
     prices the larger half of every run.
  2. **E3 after it**, still worth building at 13.8% - it is exact, its gate is bit-equality, and it
     compounds with whatever #108 finds.
  3. **E4 never.** Measured and dead.
