## 2. S224: improvable, small, and the leading cause of what is unexplained

**The record.** 7u read -0.362 at W0.02 and -0.200 at W0.01 (results-7u.txt l.253, l.546). Step 2 read -0.200
(results-recheck-7u.txt l.5). 7u-F read -0.181 and -0.281 (l.37-38). Each reading is 1.1 to 2.2 se. S224 was named as
one of 7u's three unsettled households, so 7u's own reading is biased. The two unselected re-measurements at W0.02
together give 181 saved and 226 lost, about 2.2 se. The record supports a small real loss. S224 has three bridge years
but the reader reads none, so CAND is DECOMP there (l.79). The loss belongs to the frozen candidate's settings, not the
package.

**Trade or improvable.** These figures are on the first 4,000 paths, with reduce-7uf.mjs's wholeScore (l.134-144)
split into its parts:

| | whole | survival | estate | cuts | run-out years | raises |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| W0.01 | -0.840 | -0.750 | -0.018 | -0.038 | +0.006 | -0.041 |
| W0.02 | -0.264 | -0.275 | +0.055 | -0.019 | +0.002 | -0.026 |

At W0.01 the survival loss buys nothing; at W0.02 it buys back a fifth of itself. A deliberate trade would buy something,
so this is an improvable decision. Its size is survival -0.28 and -0.18 on 16,000 paths, with the other parts -0.09 at
most. That is under D4's 0.5 per household, so by the stopping rule S224 alone owes no grid or cliff work.

**The whole-score figure.** reduce-7uf.mjs l.197 scores the whole on the first 4,000 paths, while survival is read on
16,000. On S224 at W0.01 the first 4,000 paths lose 0.750 points of survival, against 0.281 on all 16,000, and that gap
produces the "3.2 se" (STATE.md l.228; the read review's finding 1). On the better figures the objective's loss is
about -0.281 - 0.09, roughly -0.37. This corrects how the figure is read, not the printed figure.

**The mechanism (trace).** S224 starts spending in year 5. At W0.01 the candidate's end-of-year-0 wealth moves 0.79 as
much as SHIP's with the market (95% of paths apart; the mean £8,600 lower). The ratio is 0.95 in year 1 (49% of paths
apart) and 1.00 again by year 4: a de-risked opening that the charge holds for a year or two. At W0.02 the opening is
SHIP's, and the candidate de-risks on 7% to 10% of paths in years 1 to 4 (ratio 0.98 to 0.99). In the same years S214,
S382, S394, S406 and S418 show nothing like it (1.00 +/- 0.01).

The loss lands mostly at S224's minimum end pot, £430,000 or 6.1 years of spending (items/NEGRID.md l.46). Of the 68
paths lost at W0.01 among the first 4,000, 46 end under the pot without running out. The net is 22 more end-pot
failures and 8 more run-outs, and the mean estate is £12,461 lower (l.83). 7u saw the same opening: "CAND opens a step
down at gap 9.0786e-6 (9.0014e-5 at 0.01) under margin 0, a tie, and the charge holds it" (deep-review-log.md l.144).

**The leading cause: a near-tie risk choice decided inside the 30-point table's own error.** The candidate leaves its
held tiers only when the move beats staying by the charge, 0.001 or a tenth of a point (solve.js l.1394-1399). S224's
tables carry errors of whole points.

For:
- At one opening, S224's world-1 table moved 3.07 points between 30 and 60 wealth points (items/NEGRID.md l.26). That
  is the size of the candidate's world-1 over-read in 7u-F (+2.96 and +3.38; l.82, l.87). In the one-pot known answer,
  the 30-point table is 3.3 points off and within 0.06 at 90 points (results-known-answer.txt l.4-5).
- At 30 points S380's log-odds read chose a de-risked opening that neither read chose at 60, and forcing it lost
  bad-world paths (items/NEGRID.md l.24, l.33). On S370 the 60-point axis moved the policy (world 0 +0.975 points,
  0.445 to 1.451; results-copyw.txt l.115) where a table fix did not (section 4).
- The choice flips with the estate weight (de-risked at W0.01 only) and buys nothing. That is what a mis-ranked
  near-tie does.

Against:
- At 120 points S224's table still sits 1.9 points under its runs in world 0, and the poor world's cells had not
  converged (items/NEGRID.md l.54-57). Its 60- and 120-point policies agreed within a quarter point (l.72). If
  resolution moves S224's policy, it does so between 30 and 60 points, and no record tests that.
- With a charge in both passes, the candidate de-risks only where the edge sits just above the charge: the selection
  bound of the 12 Oct outside report (escalations/12-Oct-opening-near-ties-report-maintainer.md l.68; grade C, not
  checked in the record). And SHIP's per-world tables price the continuation as if the world were known (ibid. l.27),
  where joint worlds' cannot adapt, so the candidate values risk less. Either predicts the same de-risk at 90 points.
- At W0.02 the opening is SHIP's and the loss is still two-thirds as large, so the opening is not all of it.

## 3. The one test that would decide it

V3's wealth ladder on S224 at both weights, with SHIP beside it. This is V4's ensemble with V3's finest setting as a
variant, which D20 (8) already adopted; no new diagnostic.
- **Runs.** The frozen candidate (e65e5e99ba49) is solved at 30, 60 and 90 wealth points, everything else as shipped, at
  W0.01 and W0.02. SHIP is solved at 30 points at both weights. Each policy is simulated on 32,000 paths of the tuning
  seed 7002 with runPolicy (V2 and 7u-F hold it to the engine), with the whole score on every path. Each solve's year-0
  move is printed beside it (from the solve, no paths needed).
- **Why 32,000 paths.** S224's discordance is 1.3% to 2.7% (l.37-38). A paired se is about 0.13 to 0.18 points at 8,000
  paths and 0.06 to 0.09 at 32,000; the effect to tell from zero is 0.2 to 0.3.
- **Read.** Paired by path, at each weight, in survival and whole score: CAND at k less CAND at 30, and CAND at k less
  SHIP at 30.
- **Outcomes.**
  - **Resolution.** At 60 and 90 points the candidate opens at the plan's tiers at W0.01, and at both weights its
    survival comes within 0.1 of SHIP's while CAND at 30 sits 0.2 or more below. Then S224's loss belongs to the 30-point
    axis. No method work is owed on S224. V3's wealth direction decides whether 30 points stands: S224 alone, moving
    under 0.5, does not fail it. Phase 4's harm rule is then set knowing that differences of 0.2 to 0.3 points at 30
    points can come from resolution. More points cost gate 5 time: about twice the solve per doubling.
  - **Method.** At 90 points the candidate still de-risks early and still sits 0.2 or more below SHIP. Then the choice
    belongs to the candidate's tier state with its charge, or to joint worlds, not to resolution. It is within D4's
    target, so no grid or cliff work is owed. S224 is recorded as the candidate's known small cost (0.2 to 0.3 points,
    nothing bought) for the maintainer's Phase 4 harm-rule call. V4's purpose test at S224's years 0 to 4 shows which
    decisions carry it. Any variant goes to Step 6 through V4, not before Phase 4.
  - **Noise.** CAND at 30 sits within 0.1 of SHIP at both weights. Then the loss does not reproduce at this power, and it
    is closed as noise at the 0.1-point level.
- **Cost.** CAND's 30-point solve of S224 took 665 s alone (results-v-candidate.txt l.13) and 1,313 to 1,500 s under
  7u-F's load. A 60-point solve takes about 2.1 times a 30-point one (the escalation, l.54), and 90 points about 3
  times. runPolicy runs at 1.9e-3 s a path-year under load (results-budget-7uf.txt l.1), about 2,500 s a policy at 32,000
  paths. The total is about 11 core-hours, roughly three hours on four cores. The W0.02 solves are V3's own S224 units.
