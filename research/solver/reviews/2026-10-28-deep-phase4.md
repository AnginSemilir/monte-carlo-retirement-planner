## 2. MINOR 2's two items: the leading cause of each

**(a) 110% the year before a failure, and 100% in the failure year.**

What the units show (CAND/0.02, `fails.mjs`):
- Of 18,276 failures before the last year, 5,399 follow a 110% year, 5,540 a year at 100 to 109%, 4,771 a cut, and 2,566
  the floor.
- The three years before a failure climb. The commonest runs are CCC, CCT, CTR and CCR (C a cut, T target, R 110%). No
  failure follows two 110% years.
- Ages at failure are mostly 75 to 94 (72%). 391 failures fall before pension access (mostly S304F, S308F and S368), none
  after a 110% year.
- SHIP is the same (27.8% follow a 110% year), so this is solving at all, not the research.

What the code does:
- A cut is charged `lambda (1 - level)^2` (costOf, l.640).
- A move that cannot pay is charged every remaining spending year at the floor's own cost (failureShortfall, l.958-961,
  l.1168). So a year of ruin costs exactly what a year at the floor costs.
- A raise's credit is weighted by the survival it leads to (l.943, l.1170).

Where survival is nil at every level, then:
- cutting now buys nothing and costs this year's charge;
- spending 100% now and failing a year sooner costs one floor year less than cutting now and failing a year later;
- the raise credit, times a tiny but positive survival, puts 110% ahead of 100%.

What the probe measured, at 23 such positions on S090 and 36 on S328F:
- The chooser's own expected survival is 0.0001% for its pick and for its best floor move.
- The pick beats the floor by 8.83e-4 and 8.89e-4. That is the floor's charge, 0.0224 x 0.04 = 8.94e-4, and nothing else.
- Wealth is 1.0 to 1.2 years of target.
- Forcing the floor saves 0 of 4,600 and 0 of 7,200 paired paths. It postpones the failure by 0.14 and 0.26 years on
  average, and spends less before it: 0.92 and 1.02 years of target against the pick's 1.10 (the levels summed to the
  failure, less the failure year's 100% fallback).

The 100% in the failure year: every move fails, so every score is -Infinity and chooseAction keeps `best = 0` (l.1681-1685).
That is the plan's draw at level 1, which buildActions puts first (l.133-135). Delivered spending is what was available at
any level, so A2 and survival are unaffected.

**Verdict on (a).** The objective working as designed. The table is right that these paths are lost, and the engine
executes the table faithfully. It is a gap in the objective's specification, not a defect in the solver, the table read or
the engine. Ruin is priced as a floor year, so the plan does not value postponing it, and the outcome cost is near nil.

**What a user would see:** on a path heading for ruin in their eighties, years of cuts, then "spend your full target",
then "spend 110%", then the money runs out with the plan still showing 100%. That belongs in Part C's presentation: when
the plan's survival from here is nil, say so instead of showing a raise. Or price ruin as 'zero' (an option the code
keeps, which makes the whole plan more cautious and would need measuring).

**(b) The floor in the first year on the 13 households retired at the start.**

Every solver unit does it (CAND and SHIP at both weights; `traj.mjs`). Year 0 is one position, so "every path" is one
decision.

On S090 the year-0 pick:
- spends 80% and moves the pension and ISA three tiers down, on all 2,000 paths;
- the best move at each spending level also sits three tiers down;
- the chooser expects 95.21% survival at 80% and 94.94% at 100%: 0.27 points for a charge of 0.089 points.

Simulated (100% at year 0 instead, the candidate after, 2,000 paths of 7002):
- 3 saved, 8 lost: -0.25 points, about 1.5 se;
- lifetime spending the same: 34.93 against 34.86 years of target.

So the cut is timing: cut while the sequence risk is greatest rather than later. At lambda 0.0224 a year at the floor is
worth 0.09 points of survival, so any cut that buys more is taken.

**Verdict on (b).** The objective working as designed: the table and the simulation agree. Not a defect.

**What a user would see:** someone who has just retired, at a target the app calls 85% safe, is told to spend 80% of it
and to move to a portfolio two or three tiers lower. Spending rises to 110% later if markets allow.

## 3. The trade-offs a user would feel: designed, at research settings

| Spending by retired year (mean level, % of target; a year after a failure counts 0) | years 0-4 | 5-9 | 10-19 | 20 on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 retired at the start: APP | 97.7 | 94.7 | 94.0 | 92.9 |
| CAND/0.02 | 82.0 | 91.0 | 99.7 | 105.4 |
| CAND/0.01 | 83.5 | 94.4 | 102.0 | 105.6 |
| 20 FIRE: APP | 99.0 | 97.4 | 95.8 | 90.4 |
| CAND/0.02 | 95.0 | 102.2 | 103.7 | 104.6 |

(`timing.mjs`.)

- **Deeper and earlier cuts.** These are the cut price lambda.
  - CAND/0.02 spends 8.88 years at the floor, against APP's 5.96 (finding 2).
  - Over the first ten retired years, CAND/0.02 averages 94.4% of target against APP's 97.3%.
  - A2's +6.5% is earned late: after year 5 for the FIRE cohort, after year 10 for the retirees.
  - The objective has no weight on when spending happens, or on being alive to spend it. Moving spending from the first
    five years to after year 20 costs it nothing.
- **The estate.** The mean estate is lower on 37 and 39 of 40, and the median end wealth on 35 and 40. The unlucky
  tenth's end wealth is higher on 40 of 40 at both weights.
  - The candidate spends the upside: 110% in 74 to 77% of retired years on paths not yet failed, APP 29%.
  - It also holds lower tiers. On the probe, S090 is below its plan's tier 26 of 36 years. S328F holds two tiers down for
    56 of 61 years, from age 37, while still saving.
  - The estate counts at weight 0.02 or 0.01 (capped at four times the opening wealth), so the raise credit, 0.095 points
    a year at 110% in the futures that survive, usually wins.
- **CAND less SHIP spending** (-0.47% at W0.02, -0.11% at W0.01) buys +0.83 and +0.97 points. At the objective's prices
  that is a good trade. The library cohort pays it (-0.93% for +0.64); the FIRE cohort does not (-0.02% for +1.02).
  Phase 4 has no arm to say which of T3, HR or 45 points does that. 7u-F's spending cost fell on the bridge households
  instead, on the earlier candidate.

None of this is mis-specified in the sense of a bug. Each is what the objective asks at lambda 0.0224, estate 0.01 to 0.02
and ruin priced as a floor year. Two are specification choices for the maintainer before Part C: the absence of any time
or mortality weight, and the ruin price. The slider defaults decide which corner users get; Phase 4 measured one.

## 6. The leading cause of what is unexplained, and the one test

What is unexplained: how the 10.7 points over the app's rules divide between what the app's rules cannot do and the
solver's own decisions. That decides what a Part C user who holds less risk, or declines risk changes, would get.

**The leading cause: the tier changes.** Every panel household holds the app's default top tiers. The app's rules keep
them there for life, and the solver moves the pension and ISA two or three tiers down for most of the plan. It is the
largest single part of the gain.

For:
- **The lead** (`split.mjs`, 1,000 paths of 7002 each; points over APP, SHIP / SHIP-NC):

  | household | SHIP | SHIP-NC |
  |---|---|---|
  | S050 | 14.1 | 11.5 |
  | S090 | 6.8 | 3.3 |
  | S188 | 12.2 | 5.0 |
  | S272 | 8.8 | 3.2 |
  | S304F | 7.5 | 4.6 |
  | S328F | 8.1 | 4.0 |
  | S334F | 8.4 | 4.1 |
  | S368 | 14.4 | 10.9 |
  | mean | 10.0 | 5.8 |

  The tiers carry 4.2 points (42%). Leaving out S050 (the pot) and S368 (the bridge), they carry 2.9 to 7.2 points on the
  other six, 39 to 64% of each household's gain.
- **The probe.** S090 opens three tiers down on every path; S328F holds two down for 91% of its years.
- **The archive.** Phase 6's edge over fixed rules was +5.03 with tiers against +0.73 without. In 6b, flexible spending
  alone was not significantly ahead of the guardrails.
- **The third tier step** alone added +0.55 on V3's panel.

Against:
- Without tiers the product's solver still gains 3.2 to 11.5 points on every one of the eight: the larger share on the mean.
- Much of that is the minimum pot (2.98 points of A1 panel-wide, about 11 of S050's) and the bridge (S368), neither of
  which needs tiers.
- The lead is SHIP, not the candidate, on 1,000 paths (about 1 point of se a household).

**The one test.** V4's ensemble with one variant: the product's solver with the consent withdrawn. It is the archived
"arm S without tier changes", on the product's own path, so no new rule.
- **Households:** all 40 of the panel. They have been read on 7003 and are now ordinary research households.
- **Seed and paths:** seed 7002, the first 8,000 paths.
- **Arms:** APP as Phase 4 runs it; SHIP as Phase 4 runs it at W0.02; SHIP-NC, the same with `riskConsent: false`. All
  scored by the engine (runPath with withTable) and paired by path.
- **Read** as reduce-phase4.mjs reads A1, pooled and per household:
  - SHIP less APP, SHIP-NC less APP, and the tiers' share, 1 - (SHIP-NC - APP)/(SHIP - APP);
  - beside each, spending delivered by retired years 0-4, 5-9 and 10 on, the years at the floor, and the end wealth's
    median and unlucky tenth.
- **Outcomes:**
  - **The tiers carry half or more.** The gain over the app's rules is chiefly moving the user's money to lower risk.
    Before Part C:
    - the consent default and the copy say so;
    - a candidate configuration that runs without consent is built and measured against SHIP-NC;
    - the headline for a user who declines is SHIP-NC's;
    - M21's Medium-tier check (the panel's plans at Medium, APP re-landed at 85%) follows, to size the gain for users
      who chose less risk.
  - **The tiers carry a third or less.** The solver's own decisions carry it. Consent is not load-bearing, M21 closes as
    a small effect, and the no-consent configuration is still needed but not for the headline.
  - **Between:** both figures go to the maintainer for the consent default.
  - **Either way:** if SHIP-NC cuts earlier or deeper than SHIP to make up for the lost de-risking, users who decline
    feel more cuts. That is reported beside the result.
- **Cost:** SHIP's solves at 30 points take 110 to 200 s, SHIP-NC's about half (S090: 111 s and 59 s). Phase 4's SHIP
  units ran the engine at about 48 ms a path; APP's is negligible. The total is about 9 core-hours, a little over two
  hours on four cores. It is EXPLORATORY, so it changes no default without a ruling naming it.

## 8. What the maintainer should know before deciding on Part C

1. The result is real. On households like the panel's, the candidate keeps about three in four of the app's failures
   from happening and delivers more lifetime spending. The research since 7u-F adds about a point of that.
2. Most of the gain comes from things the app's rules never do:
   - lower the portfolio's risk tier: the largest single part, about two-fifths on the lead;
   - steer to the minimum pot: about 3 of the 10.7 points;
   - cut early and hard, and spend more later.
3. Users will feel it as:
   - 80% of target and a portfolio two or three tiers lower at retirement;
   - for retirees, about 82% of target on average over the first five years against the app's 98%;
   - raises to 110% later and a smaller median estate;
   - on a failing path, a raise the year before the money runs out.

   These are the objective's own choices at research settings (lambda, the estate weight, ruin priced as a floor year).
   Part C's slider defaults have not been chosen and decide them.
4. The size does not carry over to plans the app's rules already carry to 95%, to users who chose less risk than the
   defaults, or to couples (the solver does not plan them).
5. As built, the candidate cannot run for a user who declines risk changes, a lever Part C's design has.
6. Gate 5 is met on four cores; on one core the candidate takes about three times the product's solve. N7 (a laptop and a
   phone) has not run.
