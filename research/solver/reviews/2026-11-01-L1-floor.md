## Findings

1. BLOCKING. The change breaks a fast-tier test that is protected, and the fix needs the maintainer. solver-app.test.mjs
   check 2 compares `solveForApp(plan)` with `solvePlan(candidatePlan(plan), CANDIDATE_OPTS)` on S126 prepared without a
   floor (l.29, l.47, l.56-59). The app side now sets the floor at 80% and the reference does not. Their menus are
   `[1,1.1,0.95,0.9,0.8]` against `[1,1.1]`, and the digests differ (5ae122e927620cd1 against fe85b2679e929e51,
   rv/check2.mjs above). So the fast tier fails on this commit: fast-skip does not skip the test, because it reads
   src/solverSettings.js. solver-app.test.mjs is in FAST (run-all.mjs l.38), so it is protected (pre-tool.mjs l.26-31).
   D53's unlock covered exactly B8's edits, and "a later edit to a protected test asks again". The draft message and
   STEP-L.md do not mention it. Fix, on the maintainer's unlock: build check 2's reference on the plan with the floor at
   `Math.round(0.8 x target)`, as solver-progress.test.mjs l.30 does, and keep the app side on the plan without one. The
   check then pins, bit for bit, both what L1 is for (the app solves a floorless plan exactly as every candidate test
   prepared it) and L2's position-1 identity. Nothing else pins either against the research's solve. A rerun of that check
   alone takes about 80 s.
2. MINOR. The test cannot tell the rounding apart. 0 of the 210 singles have a fractional `0.8 x target`, so a
   `Math.floor` or `Math.ceil` in `prepareForSolve` passes check 1. The code's expression is identical to
   phase4-panel.mjs l.98, and the difference would be under a pound. Fix: one case with a target such as 20001, which must
   give 16001.
3. MINOR. Check 1's "your plan keeps a floor of 0" tests `normalizePlan` alone, which L1 does not touch
   (solver-floor.test.mjs l.31), so it cannot fail. The property holds by the code (question 1). A regression that wrote
   the prepared floor back into the plan's state would not be caught by it. Adding `spending.floorSpend` to
   solver-config-ui.cjs's KEEP list (the saved plan's fields unchanged) would catch one.
4. MINOR, for the maintainer's awareness; the code matches the ruling. "Where the plan sets none" keeps any own floor,
   including one at or above the target. App.jsx l.1595-1598 reads a floor equal to the target as fixed spending and
   clamps it to the target. Such a plan solves with the menu `[1,1.1]` (productLevels(1)), which is F1's no-cut
   condition, and the tab says "as little as 100% of your target (the floor in your plan)". A floor between 0 and 80% is
   untested too ("other floors measured before offered"). No screen writes `floorSpend` (grep of src/), so only an
   imported plan can carry one.
5. MINOR, for later (Phase 9's comparison and its wording), not a defect of L1. Phase 4's arm A ran the app's rules with
   the floor at 80% too. phase4-panel.mjs `planOf` sets it for every arm (l.98), its `appPlan` turns the guardrails on
   (l.102-104), and the engine floors the guardrails' cuts there (App.jsx l.2334-2337). "Your plan" in the app keeps a
   floor of 0, so its guardrails cut with no bottom. Any in-app comparison of the solver with "your plan" is therefore not
   the comparison A1's +10.7 points measured.
