## Findings

1. MINOR. Claim 5 deserves a line in the report as a possible concern. R3's CLEAR does not show the problem L5 was built
   to find, which is a large cost pushing a year's pension draw through the bands (audit-costs.mjs l.3-4, M12). R3 reads
   survival only. In every informative cell CANDD is 3.1 to 26.9 points ahead of APP, and that includes the candidate's
   general gain over the app's rules (Phase 4's A1). So the read shows that the candidate beats the app's rules on these
   32 cells, each a single cost of 10% or 30% in the third or tenth retired year of the plan. That is the comparison the
   switch needs. It does not show that the candidate pays a cost well, and it does not cover costs in working years or
   several costs.

   The evidence:
   - Per-path figures. The printed figure is the mean over all paths, including paths that failed earlier under one arm.
     On S368, APP fails 435 paths before pension access in year 7, before the cost at t 9 (my APP re-run). On the paths
     where both arms survive the gap is larger: S368 c30y10 +£70,916 a path (2,384 paths) and S188 c30y3 +£16,172 (2,092).
   - Spread. On S368 c30y10, where both survive, CANDD's cost-year tax runs from £69,716 to £95,916 (10th to 90th
     percentile, median £82,870). APP's median is £3,301 and its 90th percentile £49,487.
   - What explains it, as far as I can tell from the code and outputs. In both cells APP takes the full 25% lump sum
     (unit labels "ISA First, lump sum" and "Bracket Fill, lump sum"). My APP re-run shows the lump held in cash going
     into the cost year: a mean of £235,133 at the end of year 8 on S368 and £228,217 at the end of year 6 on S188. APP
     pays the cost in its cost order: cash, GIA, ISA, then pension. The candidate has no lump-sum route: every move has
     `lump: false` (solve.js l.153, B12). It pays a cost in the move's own draw order (`costSteps: steps`, solve.js
     l.151; bridge.js l.40), so the cost's pension part is phased, with 25% tax-free on each draw. Drawing the whole
     £285,000 from S368's pension that way would cost about £141,000 in tax (E.incomeTax, Scotland). So CANDD paid part
     of the cost from the pension and part from other wrappers. The audit does not record which wrapper paid, so this is
     a lead, not a decomposition. Do not write "caused by".
   - Size over a lifetime. The cost year overstates the lifetime gap: after its lump sum, APP's later pension draws are
     fully taxable. L5 records neither lifetime tax nor the estate. Lifetime spending delivered on the paths where both
     survive is higher under CANDD: +£25,748 a path on S368 c30y10 and +£31,759 on S188 c30y3 (the units' `delivered`
     arrays; the reducer does not print these).
   - How to settle it. Record the cost year's draws by wrapper (gross pension, taxable pension, ISA, GIA, cash) in
     audit-costs. Or trace CANDD on S368 c30y10 alone (one 45-point solve). Either would show whether M12's remedy, a
     separate cost order on the menu, would reach this case at all: if the candidate's non-pension wrappers are nearly
     empty in that year, no order avoids the pension.

2. MINOR. Claim 4 holds, but the mechanism should be stated, because the S368 y3 cells test whether the household can
   bridge to its pension, not how either policy handles a cost.
   - The setup. S368 is 50 and retired, and its pension opens at 58 (ctx.nmpa 58, t 8). The pension is £807,500 of its
     £950,000. Outside the pension it holds £142,500 (ISA £76,000, GIA £19,000, cash ISA £47,500); under APP that is a
     mean of £115,409 at the end of year 1. The cost falls at t 2, age 52.
   - What the engine does with a cost it cannot pay. It pays through the cost order. The pension gives nothing before
     access (drawPension l.2176). The unpaid rest becomes unmetCost (l.2228) and goes into unmetDemand (l.2495), which
     sets preNmpaInsolvent (l.2497). runPath fails the path in that year (audit-phase4.mjs l.98). The cost is not
     deferred and is not taken from the locked pension.
   - My APP re-run on c10y3. All 2,998 failures are before access: 1,489 in the cost year itself and the rest in years
     3 to 7. The pension, £912,000 on average at the end of year 1, is untouched.
   - c30y3. £285,000 is more than everything outside the pension, so every path fails in year 2 under any policy (my
     re-run: 3,000 of 3,000 fail in year 2). The cell has no discordant path and can show neither a loss nor a gain. It
     should be reported as uninformative, not as "the same under both arms". Keeping it in the Holm family of 32 costs
     nothing here.
   - "Sinks S368" therefore means: the household cannot pay the cost and bridge to 58 from the money it can reach, and the
     engine counts a shortfall before access as a failure for the whole plan.
   - Other cells with the cost before access. The FIRE three also take their y3 cost at 54, before 58.
   - S368's y10 gains are mostly the bridge, not the cost. In c10y10 and c30y10, APP fails 436 and 435 paths before access
     in year 7, before the cost (14.5% of paths, against CANDD's +14.8 and +20.0).

3. MINOR. audit-costs.mjs does not run `runPolicy` beside the engine on CANDD. Phase 4 (audit-phase4.mjs l.234-242),
   audit-curve.mjs (l.124-134) and L4's audit-levers.mjs (l.113-118) all do; STEP-L's L5 section does not ask for it. The
   engine is the score, so R3's survival figures stand. But a gap between the solver's model and the engine in a cost

   cover the state, but on 20 paths and not on these households.
