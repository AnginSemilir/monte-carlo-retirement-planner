Findings:

1. BLOCKING. The write-up's bound on the speed exception holds for load on units 0 to 3, not for the time the look adds.
   The same file shows that time measured low, by about the margin to the bar.
   - The rule reads `x1.219` against `x1.25` (`results-tie-c2.txt` l.24). By the rule's measure there is no trade, and
     that reading stands.
   - What must not stand is this claim: "Even raised above every other couple's, they lift the median to at most
     `x1.240`, still under the bar. That bound rests on units 4 to 11 having run on a quiet machine" (report l.59-62). The
     same goes for "BELOW the bar x1.25 however much load their CANDS arms carried" (`results-tie-c2-beside.txt` l.16).
   - Units 4 to 11 did run on a quiet machine (confirmed above). Each process ran the arms in the order CANDS, HOLD,
     LOOKHOLD, TIE4.
   - On those units, HOLD against CANDS reads `x0.991`, `x1.005`, `x0.985`, `x0.994`, `x0.961`, `x0.974`, `x0.989` and
     `x0.983` (beside l.6-13). The median is `x0.987`, and seven of eight are below one. Yet HOLD made CANDS's every move
     (its outcomes are byte for byte CANDS's) and checked the hold besides.
   - TIE4, which does the first rule's work on top, has a median of `x0.968`, with seven of eight below one.
   - So the first arm's wall clock reads 1% to 3% slow on a quiet machine, whether from warm-up, the order or drift.
     LOOKHOLD ran third.
   - Recomputed from the units (`timing.mjs`):

     | Measure | Median |
     |---|---|
     | The rule's median, as read | `x1.2195` |
     | Rescaled by the quiet HOLD against CANDS | `x1.236` |
     | Rescaled by the quiet TIE4 against CANDS | `x1.260` |
     | LOOKHOLD against TIE4, both run warm, over the twelve | `x1.255` |
     | Units 0 to 3 set above every other couple, against HOLD | `x1.241` |
     | Units 0 to 3 set above every other couple, against TIE4 | `x1.292` |

   - The twelve's median falls below the bar through the library's four (`x0.96` to `x1.04`), where the look changes the
     pick at 106 to 201 decisions a couple, against 684 to 945 on the young eight.
   - The bar therefore lies within the timing's own systematic effect of the reading. `x1.240` bounds the measured ratio,

   - **To answer it:** the write-up says, beside the outcome and before the change of default:
     - the rule's reading (`x1.219`);
