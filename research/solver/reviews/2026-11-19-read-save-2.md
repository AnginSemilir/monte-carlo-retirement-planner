## Findings

1. **MINOR: the bands beside S308F's harm are the levels its policy set, and two-fifths of the harm lies in the years after
   a path runs out. There the split moves S308F's failures from the bridge to late life. The write-up and
   DECISIONS-PENDING.md say neither.**
   - **The claim.**
     - Report lines 47 and 48: "it spends more in its first retired years and less after", with `+1.948`, `-0.795` and
       `-0.316` points a year.
     - Report lines 51 to 53 frame what is open as "why that policy spends less later from that state".
     - DECISIONS-PENDING.md: "It spends more in its first six retired years and less after ... Why the same retirement
       policy then spends less is not shown".
     - Beside them: "its survival is level, `-0.050` (30 saved, 32 lost)".
   - **What the bands measure.**
     - `save-beside.mjs` section 7 bands `level`. In `runPath` (audit-phase4.mjs 97) that is
       `round(100 x row.targetSpend / schedule)`, where `row.targetSpend` is the year's living target as the move sets it
       (engine.mjs 2410 to 2413, 2477). It is not what the path pays.
     - The filter `p > 0 && q > 0` ("where both arms spend") keeps every retired path-year: none of S308F's 4,000 x 46 has
       a level of 0 in either arm, so the counts printed are paths x years (24,000, 40,000, 120,000).
     - So the bands include the years after a path has run out, where the level is set and not paid.
   - **S308F's loss, split from the unit files** (`spend-split-se.mjs`, `split-pre-post.mjs`). CANDS less CANDD, a path,
     se by path:
     - lifetime spending delivered: `-£4,904` (se `1,103`). That is the levels set times its flat target of `£40,800`,
       `-£2,338` (se `900`), less unmet spending, which rises by `£2,567` (se `801`).
     - Split at each path's first failure year in either arm, which falls on 120 paths:
       - before it, `-£2,923` (se `871`);
       - from it on, `-£1,981` (se `661`).
     - Before either arm fails, the bands are `+2.008`, `-0.817` and `-0.377` points a path-year (`bands-solvent.mjs`). So
       "more early, less later" holds for the spending paid, and carries three-fifths of the loss.
   - **Where the rest comes from** (`fails2.mjs`; the bridge is years 15 to 20, the reader's years, before its pension
     opens at year 21):
     - CANDD's 88 failures: 50 in the bridge and 38 after it. CANDS's 90: 3 in the bridge and 87 after.
     - CANDS keeps 21 of CANDD's bridge failures and delays 26 of them.
     - It loses 32 paths CANDD keeps, all after the bridge, with `£269,737` unmet on each: `+£2,158` a path over the 4,000.
     - On the 29 paths both arms lose after the bridge, it leaves `£325,826` unmet against `£138,907`: `+£1,355` a path.
     - So the count of failures is level, but the split trades S308F's bridge failures for late-life ruin, and leaves more
       unpaid.
   - **The same split shows the bands cannot stand for the spending effect elsewhere.** S310F's lifetime gain (`+£6,667`,
     se `3,067`) is all in the run-out years: `-£4,073` (se `2,194`) before the first failure and `+£10,740` (se `2,188`)
     from it on. Its bands in section 7 are `+2.250`, `-1.053` and `-0.301`.
   - **Why it matters.** B7 sends the harm "with every effect beside it". As written, a reader takes S308F's harm for a
     shift of spending from late to early under a level survival. Two-fifths of it is deeper ruin on the paths that run
     out, from failures moved out of the bridge into late life. The units show this; no decomposition of its cause is
     needed to say it.
   - **To answer it.**
     - Beside the harm, give the split: `-£2,923` in the years both arms still pay, where the bands' shape holds, and
       `-£1,981` from the first failure year on. Give the failures' move from the bridge (50 to 3) to late life (38 to 87),
       with the unmet spending's `+£2,567`.
     - Say what is open as both parts, not "why it spends less later" alone.

       drop "where both arms spend" or make the filter mean it.
