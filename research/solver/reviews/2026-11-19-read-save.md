   and builds its first obstacle to a v2 on that.**

     - Nothing measured it for the save copies. `save-split` check 5 compares the skip on and off, both on the whole menu
       (test lines 297 to 308).
   - **The code.**
     - In a year whose copies are not live, `AT` is the menu before the copies (`splitCounts`, solve.js 2962 to 2966).
       The copies come after every base move (`withSaveCopies`, 187 to 191).
     - All 14 move loops of the backward pass run to `AT` (solve.js 1100 to 1700), and a shortlist takes a move only below
       `AT` (`coarseAdd`, 3071).
     - A copy's neighbours carry its `save` (`stKey` 3039, `restKey` 3049), so a base move's widening stays among base
       moves.
     - One way remains for a copy to touch such a year: source 'T', the move the cell stored the year after (`coarseMask`,
       3077). When that year is live and stored a copy, the copy is refused, and the cell's shortlist loses that source.
       That is the windfall's case: the years before its deposit year.
     - Here that cannot happen. On all 27 households the working years with a choice run from year 0 to the year before
       retirement, without a gap (each unit's `workT`, 0 to 4 or 0 to 14). So no year without a choice comes before a
       year with one.
     - The bridge reader's adaptive reference keeps the first move of each draw order and tier pair (adaptive.js 73), so
       the copies drop out of it.
   - **The probes** (no paths; `solveCandidate` with the audit's options, with and without `saveSplit: true`, both on
     anchors).
     - At the run's own settings (45 points, the candidate's lattice), each year's tables digested over every world, layer
       and table:
       - S188 (399,040 cells on shortlists in each solve): the retired years 5 to 40, 44,089,920 entries, are the same;
         years 0 to 4 differ;
       - S308F (604,710 cells): the retired years 15 to 60, 56,337,120 entries, are the same; years 0 to 14 differ.
     - At `save-split`'s light settings, every entry compared:
       - S188: 0 of 3,919,104 retired-year entries differ (540,576 of 544,320 in its working years);
       - S308F: 0 of 5,007,744 (1,506,267 of 1,632,960);
       - S244: 0 of 4,027,968 (1,593,804 of 1,632,960).
   - **What follows.**
     - Outside the working years CANDS's tables are CANDD's to the bit, and its chooser scores the same moves there
       (`movesAt`). So in retirement the two arms run one policy.
     - They part there only through the state each brings to retirement. That is the split's working-year choices and what
       follows from them.
     - So the gains are not the skip's. "CANDS also differs a little from CANDD in other years" is untrue of this run, and
       "measure the skip's part" has nothing to measure.

       shortlists are drawn from a menu three times as long as CANDD's: an approximation acting on the two arms
