## Summary

- **PASS stands,** and is sound for what it tested. The gain replicates out of sample, the factorial's open approximation
  (anchors) is measured nil, and neither guard flags a household.
- **What the gain is made of:**
  - the working-year choice alone, mostly the pension first;
  - half of it paths clearing the minimum pot at the plan's end, the pension counted gross;
  - on 11 of the 24 fifteen-year households, lower-risk tiers before retirement, bought with the relief. These hold the
    largest gains and 10 of the 11 falls in the mean pot.
- **The leading cause of what is unexplained** (the employees' 87%): the price the engine puts on an employee's pension
  pound. That is salary sacrifice relieved of employee NIC, in every year and for every employee. Only CANDS reads that
  price: a working single's CANDD never prices its contributions.
- **The one test.** The 30 employees' split priced on income tax relief alone, on seed 7025's first 4,000 paths against
  the registered anchors pair. About 5.5 core-hours.
- **The change of default should:**
  - hold back ISA first for an employee: it moves the employer's part of the pension figure as the employee's own;
  - put the doubled path to the maintainer as the trade;
  - correct the speed mode's entry (the speed mode does nothing for a working saver);
  - carry the identity and the reach.
- **Open items:** no row moves; three lines open; D134 (3)'s cap moves onto the split's path.

## 1. What the result says, and what it does not

It says:
- **The split gains, out of sample, on the menu that ships.**
  - v2: `+0.495` (`+0.465` to `+0.526`) by path over 40 held-out households, with no household below nought (S336F
    `+0.019` the least). Spending `+0.4226%`. D34 flags none, and part 3 fails none (S280's `-0.0887%` the one fall beyond
    noise).
  - The factorial, 27 other households on seed 7002 and on anchors: `+0.398` (by path se `0.0345`), spending `+0.4233%`.
  - Anchors against the whole menu: `+0.000` without the split and `+0.004` with it. The approximation the factorial left
    open is nil.
  - S308F's harm in the factorial (`-0.250%`) did not recur. Part 3, as sized, would catch a loss of that size.
- **The working years alone part the arms.** Outside them CANDS scores CANDD's moves (`splitCounts`, `movesAt`), so its
  tables there are CANDD's, as the factorial's probe and chooser check showed. The arms differ only in the state each
  retires with.
- **Mostly the pension first.** It is 77.5% of the employees' decisions from 2029, and nearly all on the library's
  households. A net pound buys:
  - 1.39 to 1.41 of pension for a basic-rate employee, 1.72 to 1.79 at the higher rate;
  - 1.25 to 1.31 for a basic-rate self-employed saver, and 2.0 to 2.5 in the personal allowance's taper.
- **Half the net gain is the minimum pot.**
  - 1,558 of the 3,171 net paths paid every year under CANDD and ended below the floor. The floor is tested on the gross
    pot (App.jsx 2862-2865 and 3001-3002; D64's `pensionDeathTaxRate` 0), so a pound of pension counts in full, and the
    pension is the wrapper the split fills.
  - 2,657 of the 3,820 saved paths fail under CANDD in the plan's last three years. The run-out half is 1,613 net paths.
- **The relief buys lower-risk tiers before retirement on 11 of the 24 fifteen-year households.** These are S300F,
  S302F, S324F, S330F, S342F, S354F, S242, S264, S266, S270 and S282, all employees.
  - Over the working years CANDS holds 0.10 to 1.20 steps further below the plan's tiers on average.
  - On S242, S300F and S302F it holds three steps from the first year. On the other eight both arms start two steps down,
    and CANDS ends at 2.33 to 2.95 against 1.96 to 2.37.
  - No five-year household and no self-employed one moves by a tenth of a step.
  - These 11 gain `+0.940` a household, against `+0.464` for the other 13 fifteen-year households.
  - Within each salary they retire with less of the relief's extra pot than the households that do not move:
    - at £37,500: `-£5,632` to `+£18,018`, against `+£24,005` and `+£26,711`;
    - at £85,000: `+£42,315` and `+£73,534`, against `+£101,955` to `+£116,082`;
    - at £67,500: `+£13,483` to `+£41,162`, against `+£79,742`.
  - There the relief is spent on safety more than kept as wealth.
- **The pot's fall is those tiers.**
  - The mean pot falls on 11 households. Ten of them are among the 11 that hold lower tiers; the eleventh of those,
    S266, has its mean pot level (`+0.025%`).
  - The other household whose mean pot falls is S336F, which saves ISA first 74.6% of the time, tiers equal: the relief
    given up.
  - S348F, the one household that holds less safe tiers under CANDS (2.19 against 2.38), has its mean pot `+23.4%`.
  - Capped at the estate cap, the pot falls by more than 0.65% only where the saving goes ISA first: S300F `-4.05%`,
    S302F `-6.05%`, S336F `-2.05%`. The rest of the fall is the upper tail given up for survival, the pattern
    `reviews/2026-11-13-deep-couples-d4b.md` found. It answers the write-up's "the deep review will look at where it
    goes".
- **ISA first is the bridge, on the younger cohort** (who retire at 52 with pension access at 58).
  - S300F gains `+1.712`, the panel's largest, using no relief.
  - S308F's pattern, bridge failures traded for late ones, shows inside the rule as wide discordance on S302F (90 saved,
    46 lost) and S336F (45, 42).
- **The tax's `+17.5%` is not a cost.** It counts no tax on pay (the reviewer's MINOR 3).

It does not say:
- **How much of the gain the law gives.** The split's lever is the price of a pension pound, and only CANDS reads it.
  - A working single's take-home is computed only once someone has retired (App.jsx 2357; model.js 333). So CANDD's
    contributions land gross whatever they cost, and the split's routes alone price them (App.jsx 3567-3583).
  - The registration says "both arms share it, so the comparison is fair within the model". That is true of the engine.
    It is not true of the error: all of it falls on CANDS.

  - The split's budget prices all of it as the employee's own sacrifice: `netOf(P0) + I0` (3569). So ISA first moves the

  - For this panel's savers, ISA first takes the pension's saving to nought. On S300F, at retirement the pension is
    `-£108,373` and the ISA `+£56,991`.
  - In law that is stopping a workplace pension, which forfeits the employer's money.
- **Its reach**, beyond what the registration and the reviewer's MINOR 4 (a) list. Those are: positions 3, 6 and 10,

  - a saver paying into one wrapper. With the pension alone, the split's one new choice is ISA first, that is, stop the
    pension (3582-3587: pension first snaps to the plan's own).
- **The time, except as a guide.** A path on anchors takes `x2.10` (`x1.84` to `x2.31`), timed four units at a time.

## 2. The leading cause of what is unexplained

What is left: why the employees carry 87.3% of the gain from 75% of the households.
- They gain `+0.577` against the self-employed's `+0.252` (by path).
- The factorial shows the same. By my count from `results-save.txt`, employees gain `+0.503` a household over 16 and the
  self-employed `+0.245` over 11: 75% of the gain.
- In v2's fifteen-year library households the employees gain `+0.832` and the self-employed `+0.267`.

**The cause: the price the engine puts on an employee's pension pound.** It is salary sacrifice relieved of employee
NIC, in every year and for every employee. That makes the pension first worth more to an employee than to a
self-employed saver in the same band. It is also worth more than the law gives an employee from April 2029, or one who
does not sacrifice.

Evidence for:
1. **Within the basic band the gap is about threefold, and the price differs by the NIC alone.** Take the households
   that save ISA first at most two-fifths of the time:
   - the basic-rate employees gain `+0.59` a household (20, both panels);
   - the basic-rate self-employed gain `+0.19` (7: S196, S208, S280, S292, S244, S316F and S328F).

   The employee's 8% NIC makes a net pound 1.39 to 1.41 of pension against 1.25 to 1.31.
   - Drawn at the basic rate with a quarter tax-free, that is 18% to 20% more than the ISA, against 6% to 11%.
   - Inside the allowance it is 39% to 41%, against 25% to 31%.
   - From April 2029 the law prices a basic-rate employee's moved pound above £2,000 as these self-employed savers' is
     priced.
2. **The gap replicates on two panels and two seeds.**
3. **The employees' gains grow with the years of the price; the self-employed's barely do.** In v2, from five to
   fifteen working years:
   - basic-rate employees grow `x4.5` (`+0.225` to `+1.010`), higher-rate `x3.7` (`+0.175` to `+0.654`);
   - the self-employed grow `x1.3` (`+0.166` to `+0.213`) and `x1.15` (`+0.280` to `+0.322`).
4. **The tier shift that carries the largest gains is the solver's answer to extra wealth.** It occurs on employees
   only.

Evidence against:
1. **The groups differ in more than the price.** The basic-rate self-employed earn £46,000 to £58,000, against the
   employees' £29,500 to £37,500, and they hold more wealth against their targets (fifteen-year: 9.9 to 11.6 years,
   against 7.0 to 9.0). They are seven households.
2. **The price alone does not set the gain.** The self-employed in the personal allowance's taper are relieved at up to
   60%, 2.0 to 2.5 of pension a net pound. They gain `+0.29` a household (v2's six, at £142,500 to £156,500) and
   `+0.42` (the factorial's five, at £124,000): no more than basic-rate employees at 1.39.
3. **v2's five-year households show no gap:** employees `+0.200`, the self-employed `+0.242`.
4. **The panel's largest gain uses no relief.** S300F, `+1.712`, saves ISA first.
5. **Nothing in the read separates the price from the plan.** Neither the cap nor an employee's route is modelled.

## 3. The one test: the employees' split at income tax relief alone

What it runs (EXPLORATORY, its rule committed before it runs):
- **The lever.** Each of the 30 employees' plans with `employmentSelf` set to self-employed, and nothing else changed.
  - For a working single with no pass-through (all 40), that flag reaches a run only through the split's price:
    - the take-home is not computed while working (App.jsx 2357; model.js 333);
    - the taxable pay's pass-through factor is 1 either way (1436-1440);
    - the split's routes are the one other reader (3567).
  - So the moved pound is priced as the law prices it for an employee without salary sacrifice, and for a salary
    sacrificer above £2,000 from April 2029.
  - For a sacrificer it is a floor: the first £2,000 a year, and the years before 2029, are priced below the law.
- **Arms.**
  - **CANDSA-T:** the split on anchors, on the flipped plan.
  - **CANDDA-T, an identity:** the candidate on the flipped plan. Its table digest must be the registered CANDDA's, and
    on each household's first 500 paths its survival, spending, pot and tax must match path for path.
  - **A control:** S280, self-employed, whose CANDSA-T must be its CANDSA.
  - Any difference is a STOP: it means the flag reaches more than the price.
- **Seed and paths.**
  - Seed 7025's first 4,000 paths, the registered CANDDA's and CANDSA's, already read. On the read's code,
    71fb4271c681, from a worktree at a83cd90b.
  - Run directly as a tracked task, since the launcher refuses a reserved seed outside its registration. That is D110's
    form, and needs the maintainer's word on the seed.
  - Otherwise seed 7002, with CANDDA and CANDSA run beside it: about 13 core-hours.
- **Recorded** as v2's audit records: every path's survival, spending, pot, tax and kind of failure. On `runPolicy`'s
  1,000 paths, each working-year save and tier pair, and the pots at retirement.

How it is read:
- **K**, the share of the gain left at that price: CANDSA-T less CANDDA, over CANDSA less CANDDA. It is pooled survival
  by path over the 30, with its interval by the delta method on the two paired changes.
- **Beside K:**
  - K by group: the 13 basic-rate employees outside the three that save ISA first most; the 14 higher-rate; the three,
    S300F, S302F and S336F;
  - the save shares from 2029;
  - the late working years' tier step;
  - the kind of failure.
- **The cause predicts:**
  - the basic-rate group keeps about half or less;
  - the higher-rate group keeps about nine-tenths;
  - the three keep more than all, since ISA first's budget rises when a pension pound costs more net;
  - pooled, K about 0.7.

  If the plans' shape is the cause instead, every group keeps nine-tenths or more.

What each outcome changes:
- **K and the basic-rate group's K both at least 0.85:** the cause fails. The employees' extra gain is their plans and the
  tiers they can afford.
  - The default goes ahead as the PASS has it.
  - The D-line says the gain holds on income tax relief alone, so the 2029 cap and the non-sacrificer are measured, not
    inferred.
- **Otherwise, K at least 0.33 with CANDSA-T's gain over CANDDA beyond noise:** the cause holds in size. Its signature
  is the basic-rate group's K below the higher-rate group's beyond its noise; without it, the report says so.
  - The PASS stands as a gain in law, but the size the app shows a working employee is partly the engine's.
  - The default goes to `main` with the law's price in the split's two routes, with its test. That is D134 (3)'s cap,
    brought forward for `saveSplitRoutes`.
  - The D-line gives both figures.
  - Whether the app asks how an employee pays in goes to the maintainer.
- **K under 0.33, or CANDSA-T's gain over CANDDA not beyond noise:** the employees' gain is mostly the price.
  - The default is held for employees, and comes back to the maintainer.
  - The self-employed's part may go alone on the maintainer's word. It is `+0.252` by path, all ten above nought, and
    already at the law's price.
  - v2's question for employees is asked again, registered, on the law's price.

Cost:
- **Run:** about 5.5 core-hours on 7025. CANDSA-T with its paths beside is about 4.9; the identity's solves and 500
  paths, and the control, about 0.6. Under two hours on four free cores, longer beside the harness.
- **Build:** half a day. A test audit importing v2's own functions, and a read with planted checks: K's arithmetic, and a
  CANDDA-T one path apart failing the identity.
- **Read:** half a day.

## 4. What the change of default should carry and hold back

1. **Hold back ISA first for an employee** (section 1).
   - **Where it acts.** On v2 it is 17% or more of the decisions on six younger employees: S300F 99.9%, S302F 99.3%,
     S336F 74.6%, S348F 34.2%, S312F 26.8% and S324F 17.0%. Those six carry 4.28 of the panel's 19.82 household-points
     (22%), and S300F alone 1.712.
   - **Inherited, not new.** The app's contribution strategies build the same budget (`accumulationEnv` 3951,
     `allocateBudget`). The split makes it the solver's default for every working saver, and this year's action on the
     page.
   - **Recommended:** until the plan records the employer's part, an employee's split offers the plan's own and the
     pension first. ISA first stays for the self-employed, as a change to the split's routes with its test and plant.
     Otherwise the maintainer takes the trade, with words saying what ISA first means.
   - **Its cost is not measured.** The six households' gains mix both routes. An arm beside the test can measure it, if
     wanted.
2. **The time, to the maintainer before the commit.**
   - **What doubles.** The path doubles on anchors. The solve rises `x1.10` (at most `x1.46`), which leaves gate 5 far
     off: the candidate's solve, split across its parts, is `x0.178` of the product's there.
   - **Phase 9's budget.** Its target for the headline is 60 s on 2,000 paths. Step P met it with about half to spare
     (S188, a working saver, 24.7 s). At `x2.1` a working saver's headline would sit near the target. Not measured.
   - **The precedent.** The maintainer has ruled each path-time trade so far (D105, D108, D109, at `x1.25`). This one is
     larger, and reaches every working saver.
   - **The lead.** Key `decisionRepeats` (2244) and the shortlist (1855) on the year's own count (`r.nAt[t]` against the
     base menu's) rather than on the menu having copies, with an identity test. The shortlist's grouping already carries
     `save` (1786), and outside the live years the copies are already out of the moves scored.
3. **The speed mode's entry.**
   - The D-line should say, from the code, that the speed mode does nothing on a working saver's path once the split is
     on, not list it as reached "by inference".
   - Until the lead in item 2 is in, the warning's "faster" (D104 (4)) is untrue for them. Under the speed mode a
     non-saver's path runs at `x0.54` (SPEED-B); a working saver's at `x2.1`.
4. **The identity and the reach.**
   - The identity on the code the change lands on: the reviewer's MINOR 4 (b).
   - The reach as section 1 widens it: the non-sacrificer and the one-wrapper saver.
5. **The pot, as the trade.** Ten of the 11 falls in the mean pot come with lower tiers held before retiring, and the
   eleventh is S336F's ISA first. It is a survival-first choice, as D116 read the household line's. Say so in the D-line
   beside the cohorts.
