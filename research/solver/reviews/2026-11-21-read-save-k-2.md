## Findings

1. **BLOCKING. The report to the maintainer sizes the pay-in question by K. The corrected write-up and the change's
   identity show that is not its size.** `DECISIONS-PENDING.md` lines 19-21 (653bb2b1): "What it leaves. An employee who
   pays in by net pay or relief at source is relieved of income tax only, in every year. That is K's price. At it, the
   panel's 30 employees keep `0.726` of the split's gain, and the basic-rate employees `0.580`. The app cannot tell the two
   kinds of employee apart, so it prices every employee as a sacrificer."
   - **What K measures.** K sets K's price in every year against the engine's price in every year (NIC relief in full,
     uncapped), both with the ISA first offered. The change prices an employee neither way.
   - **What the change does instead.** Under (B), a non-sacrificer is priced as a sacrificer. From April 2029 that price
     gives K's pension first on all 30, which is a non-sacrificer's own price in law:
     - the write-up says so (lines 63-71);
     - the change's identity shows it (`results-save-default-identity.txt` part 2: 260 of 260, the largest gap £0);
     - my own loop on the build agrees: 260 of 260 from 2029, and the cap changes nothing in the 90 years before it.
   - **So what (B) leaves** a non-sacrificer on these plans is the routes of 2026 to 2028: 90 of their 350 working years,
     unmeasured. It is not the quarter of the gain that `0.726` reads as, nor the `0.42` that `0.580` reads as.
   - **Why it must not stand.** This is the framing my first look's finding 1 said must not stand: "It spreads the stakes
     of the pay-in question across the whole gain. On these plans they sit in the three years before April 2029." The
     write-up now carries that fact. The entry the maintainer chooses (A) or (B) from does not, and what (B) leaves is the
     case it makes for (A).
   - **A second error.** The entry's "the basic-rate employees `0.580`" is the 13 basic-rate employees outside the three.
     The panel's basic-rate employees are 16: the three (S300F, S302F, S336F, all on £29,500) are basic-rate too, and their
     K is `0.899`.
   - **Fix.** Put the write-up's facts in the entry, with finding 2's condition:
     - as the app prices it, from April 2029 an employee's pension first is K's on these 30, so the question decides the
       2026-2028 routes alone, unmeasured;
     - K is the share of the gain at K's price against the engine's price, every year, with the ISA first offered; it is
       not the change's arm;
     - in law the cap counts the employee's own sacrifice, which the app does not record.

2. **MINOR. The write-up states a result "under the law" that holds only as the app prices it.** Lines 63-71: "Every one
   of the 30 plans pays more than that into the pension [...] So under the cap, a further pound of sacrifice from April
